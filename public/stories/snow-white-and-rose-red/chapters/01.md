# The Two Rose Trees

Once there was a mother who lived with her two daughters in a cottage beside a great forest.

In the garden grew two rose trees.

One covered itself in white blossoms.

The other bloomed bright red.

The sisters were named after them: Snow-White and Rose-Red.

They were alike in many ways and different in others.

Snow-White liked quiet mornings, books by the fire and listening for birds in the trees.

Rose-Red liked racing through meadows, climbing over fallen logs and coming home with leaves in her hair.

But wherever one went, the other was never far away.

<!-- block:always-together -->
“We will always look after each other,” Snow-White would say.

“Always,” Rose-Red would answer.

Their mother would smile.

“And whatever one of you has, you share with the other.”

So they shared everything.

Baskets of berries.

Warm blankets.

Chores.

Secrets.

And when one of them was frightened, the other always reached for her hand.

The forest around their cottage was deep, but the sisters knew its paths well.

They gathered mushrooms beneath old trees, followed deer tracks through the ferns and listened to the brook tumbling over smooth stones.

Even so, there were places in the forest they had never seen.

And there were secrets among the trees that neither sister knew about yet.
