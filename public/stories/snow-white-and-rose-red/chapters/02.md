# The Winter Visitor

One snowy evening, while the family sat beside the fire, there came a heavy knock at the cottage door.

Rose-Red opened it.

Then she jumped backwards.

A huge black bear stood outside.

Snow covered his head and shoulders.

“Please don’t be frightened,” said the bear. “I mean you no harm. I am half frozen. May I warm myself by your fire?”

Their mother looked at him carefully.

Then she nodded.

“Come in.”

The bear squeezed through the doorway and lay beside the hearth.

Snow-White brushed snow from his thick fur.

Rose-Red brought him an old blanket, although he was much too large for it.

Soon the bear was warm and comfortable.

<!-- block:a-new-friend -->
At first the sisters kept their distance.

But the bear was gentle.

He told funny stories.

He let Rose-Red climb over his back.

He listened while Snow-White read aloud.

When he growled, it sounded more like a rumbling kettle than anything frightening.

The next morning he thanked them and disappeared into the forest.

But the following evening he returned.

And the evening after that.

All winter long the bear visited the cottage.

By spring, he felt like an old friend.

<!-- block:spring-goodbye -->
One morning the bear stood at the open door.

Sunlight shone across the melting snow.

“I must leave for a while,” he said.

“Why?” asked Rose-Red.

“There is something in the forest I must protect.”

Snow-White noticed that the bear suddenly looked serious.

“Will you come back?”

The bear smiled.

“When I can.”

Then he padded away between the trees.

As he passed through the doorway, his fur caught for a moment on the latch.

For the briefest instant Snow-White thought she saw something golden shining beneath it.

Then the bear was gone.
