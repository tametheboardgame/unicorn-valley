# The Light That Came Home

The next evening Rowan did not go to the woods alone.

He brought the baker with a flour-dusted lantern, the gardener with a green glass lamp, the racing foals with two tiny lamps tied safely to their saddlebags, and Quill carrying a blue reading light.

"Did you know this would happen?" Rowan asked.

Quill smiled. "I hoped."

<!-- block:lantern-chain -->
They reached the quiet clearing together. One by one, they placed their lights where the old mirror could catch them. Gold crossed green. Green crossed blue. Little beams bounced through the cloudy glass until the ancient lantern shone like a captured star.

Far away, at the edge of the village, the unlit lantern answered. Its silver glow became gold.

The whole path between village and woods lit up, lantern by lantern, as though the light were travelling home.

<!-- block:shared-path -->
After that night, the last lantern was included in the lamplighter's round.

Visitors sometimes asked why it stood so far from the houses.

Rowan would tell them, "It marks the beginning of a path."

If they asked where the path went, he would add, "That depends on who goes with you."

And on evenings when somebody in the village felt lost, lonely or unsure, there was usually a friend willing to walk with them as far as the lantern at the edge of the woods.
