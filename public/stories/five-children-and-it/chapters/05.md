# No Wings

The church tower became much less exciting without wings.

It became colder too.

Jane wanted tea.

Robert wanted to know why nobody had checked whether the door opened before sunset.

Cyril said this was not helpful.

Anthea made everyone stop arguing.

“We need somebody to hear us.”

They shouted.

No answer.

They shouted again.

Birds flew from the roof.

Still no rescue.

<!-- block:someone-hears -->
At last lights appeared in the nearby house.

A door opened.

The children shouted together.

This time someone heard.

The local clergyman came to investigate, followed by other adults who were understandably surprised to discover four children locked on top of a church.

The door was opened from below.

The children descended.

Questions followed.

Many questions.

How had they reached the tower?

Why had nobody seen them climb?

Why were they so far from home?

The children tried giving answers that did not include an ancient sand-fairy.

The answers were not very convincing.

<!-- block:home-again -->
Eventually they were taken home.

Martha was furious and relieved in equal amounts.

The children were sent indoors and told that tomorrow they were absolutely not going wandering around the countryside.

That should have prevented another wish.

It did not.

Before bedtime, the children discussed what had happened.

“Flying was brilliant,” said Robert.

“Until the wings vanished,” said Jane.

“That's the problem,” said Anthea. “The wish always ends. We keep forgetting to plan for afterwards.”

Cyril nodded.

Their list of rules was becoming quite long.

Unfortunately, having rules and remembering them at the right moment were two completely different things.
