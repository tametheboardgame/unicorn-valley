# Bigger Than the Baker's Boy

The next wish began with an argument.

A baker's delivery boy passed while the children were playing.

Robert teased him.

The boy teased Robert back.

The quarrel became unpleasant.

Robert ended up bruised and furious.

“He only won because he's bigger than me!”

The children walked towards the gravel pit.

Robert kicked at the sand.

“I wish I was bigger than him.”

Something furry moved beneath his hand.

The others groaned.

<!-- block:a-very-large-robert -->
Robert grew.

And grew.

And kept growing.

His clothes grew with him.

Within moments he towered over everyone.

He was not simply bigger than the baker's boy.

He was enormous.

At first Robert was delighted.

Then he tried walking along the road.

People stared.

Some ran away.

Others followed him.

A man wanted to exhibit him as the largest boy in England.

Robert suddenly understood what it felt like to be treated as a curiosity instead of a person.

<!-- block:big-enough-to-regret-it -->
He found the baker's boy again.

Robert could have frightened him badly.

For a moment he wanted to.

Then he looked down from his ridiculous height.

Winning an argument because you were magically ten feet tall did not seem much better than winning because you happened to be bigger in the first place.

Robert settled for making it very clear that the quarrel was over.

The rest of the day was spent avoiding crowds and waiting for sunset.

When Robert finally returned to his normal size, everyone was relieved.

“So,” Cyril said, “did being bigger help?”

Robert rubbed his ordinary-sized nose.

“Not really.”

Another rule joined the list.

If your wish begins with “I'll show them,” it is probably worth thinking again.
