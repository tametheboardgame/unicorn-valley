# Beautiful as the Day

Cyril, Anthea, Robert and Jane were staying in the country with their baby brother, whom everyone called the Lamb.

Their new house stood near a gravel pit.

This was excellent news.

A gravel pit might look dull to grown-ups, but to children with spades it can become a fort, a desert, a mine, a ruined city or almost anything else.

One morning the four older children went digging.

Robert struck something soft beneath the sand.

Something moved.

Then two long eyes appeared on stalks.

A furry body wriggled upwards.

The children dropped their spades.

<!-- block:the-thing-in-the-sand -->
“What are you?” asked Anthea.

The creature looked offended.

It was a Psammead, it explained. A sand-fairy.

Long ago, before people built houses everywhere, Psammeads had granted wishes.

“Real wishes?” asked Jane.

“Real enough to cause trouble,” said the Psammead.

It puffed itself up until its body became round and tight.

The children stared.

They were allowed one wish a day.

Whatever they wished for would disappear at sunset.

That sounded safe.

It was not.

<!-- block:first-wish -->
After a great deal of arguing, they wished to be “as beautiful as the day.”

The Psammead granted it.

For a few minutes the children were delighted.

Their hair shone. Their clothes seemed suddenly elegant. They looked like storybook princes and princesses.

Then they went home.

Martha, who looked after them, did not recognise them.

She shut the door.

The children shouted their names through the window.

Martha threatened to fetch help if the strange children did not go away.

No amount of explaining worked.

The wish had changed them so completely that nobody knew who they were.

Hungry, thirsty and increasingly cross, they spent the afternoon waiting for the sun to go down.

At sunset the beauty vanished.

Their familiar faces returned.

Martha recognised them immediately.

That evening the children agreed on their first rule of wishing.

A wish could come true perfectly and still be a terrible idea.
