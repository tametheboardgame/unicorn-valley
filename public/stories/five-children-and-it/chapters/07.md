# A Siege and Bed

The children hurried to the battlements.

Below, the attacking soldiers shouted challenges.

Cyril immediately became extremely interested.

“A proper siege!”

Anthea was less impressed.

“We haven't even had dinner.”

The castle had walls, towers and gates.

What it did not have was an army.

The only defenders were four hungry children.

<!-- block:defending-the-castle -->
They improvised.

They rolled harmless objects from the walls.

They shouted instructions to one another as if hundreds of soldiers were waiting nearby.

Robert ran from tower to tower pretending to command invisible troops.

For a while it was exciting.

Then the attackers began trying seriously to enter.

The children remembered that a pretend siege is much more enjoyable than a real one.

They barricaded doors.

They argued about what to do.

Jane asked when the sun was going down.

Everyone looked west.

Not soon enough.

<!-- block:when-the-magic-ends -->
The children held out.

They discovered that they could be inventive when frightened.

Cyril planned.

Anthea noticed things the others missed.

Robert did the jobs nobody else could reach.

Jane kept everyone from giving up.

At last the light changed.

The sun slipped lower.

The castle walls shimmered.

The attacking army disappeared.

Stone corridors became familiar rooms.

The impossible fortress became their ordinary house again.

Martha appeared, still convinced the children had spent the day inside.

Soon they were being sent to bed.

Cyril lay awake thinking.

A day earlier, wings had seemed like the best wish imaginable.

Then a castle had seemed even better.

Perhaps the problem was not finding the right exciting wish.

Perhaps exciting wishes were the problem.
