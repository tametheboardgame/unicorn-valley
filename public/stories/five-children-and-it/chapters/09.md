# Grown Up

The Lamb was still the youngest member of the family.

This meant he was carried when tired, helped when stuck and forgiven for things the older children would never have escaped.

One afternoon someone complained that everything would be easier if the Lamb were older.

The words became a wish.

By now, everyone should have known better.

<!-- block:the-lamb-grows-up -->
The toddler vanished.

In his place stood a young man.

He had neat clothes, grown-up manners and absolutely no memory of being the Lamb.

The children stared.

Their baby brother looked at them as though they were a collection of annoying small people.

“What are you all doing here?”

Anthea tried explaining.

He did not believe her.

He wanted lunch.

He wanted to go into town.

He had opinions about everything.

Worst of all, he behaved as though he had always been grown up.

<!-- block:keeping-him-nearby -->
The children could not let him disappear into the world.

At sunset he would become a toddler again.

If that happened on a train, in a shop or halfway across the county, explaining it would be impossible.

So they delayed him.

They distracted him.

They invented reasons not to leave.

When he wanted to ride away, they found ways to keep him nearby.

The grown-up Lamb became increasingly annoyed.

The children became increasingly desperate.

At last they reached home just as the sun went down.

Martha came outside.

To the children, a grown man was standing beside them.

To Martha, it was simply the Lamb.

She scooped him into her arms.

The sun disappeared.

The young man vanished.

Their sleepy little brother returned.

Anthea hugged him.

Cyril looked thoughtful.

“Maybe growing up is something that works better slowly.”

Nobody disagreed.
