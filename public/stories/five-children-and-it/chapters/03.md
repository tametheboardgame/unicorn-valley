# Being Wanted

The Lamb was having a difficult morning.

He spilled things.

He grabbed things.

He wriggled when anyone tried to dress him.

By the time breakfast was over, everyone was tired.

Robert said something he did not really mean.

“I wish somebody else wanted the Lamb for a change.”

There was a silence.

The four children looked at one another.

Then they ran for the gravel pit.

Too late.

The Psammead had heard the wish.

<!-- block:everybody-wants-the-lamb -->
At first nothing happened.

Then they took the Lamb outside.

A woman passing on the road stopped.

“What a lovely child!”

She wanted to carry him.

A family they met wanted to take him home.

Another traveller insisted the Lamb was exactly the little boy they had always wanted.

Everywhere the children went, someone wanted their baby brother.

Not simply admired him.

Wanted him.

The children clung to the Lamb and hurried away.

<!-- block:keeping-him-safe -->
The afternoon became a long chase from one well-meaning stranger to another.

Cyril tried making plans.

Robert tried arguing.

Jane nearly cried.

Anthea kept hold of the Lamb whenever she could.

The wish had sounded convenient when the Lamb was throwing breakfast.

Now the thought of somebody actually taking him made all four children feel sick.

They wanted him back with them.

They wanted his sticky hands and loud complaints and badly timed wriggling.

They wanted all of it.

At sunset the strange power of the wish ended.

People stopped trying to claim the Lamb.

The children carried him home.

Robert watched his little brother fall asleep.

“I suppose we always wanted him,” he said.

“Of course we did,” said Anthea.

They added another rule.

Never make a wish about someone else just because you are annoyed with them.
