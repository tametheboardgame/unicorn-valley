# The Game Comes to Life

By now the children had begun to understand that wishes could turn games into trouble.

Unfortunately, understanding something does not always stop you doing it.

One morning they were playing an elaborate adventure.

There were explorers, warriors, secret camps and daring escapes.

The game became louder.

The children argued about who was winning.

Someone said it would be much more exciting if the adventure were real.

The Psammead was nearby.

That was all it needed.

<!-- block:not-a-game-anymore -->
The imagined visitors appeared.

They were not toys.

They were not actors.

They were a proud band of warriors who had somehow stepped into the children's afternoon from another time and place.

They looked around at the house, the road and the strange modern world.

The children immediately regretted everything.

Pretending to be surrounded was fun.

Actually being surrounded was not.

Anthea's first thought was the Lamb.

She arranged for Martha to take him safely away for the afternoon.

Then the four older children tried to deal with the consequences of their wish.

<!-- block:talking-instead-of-fighting -->
At first both sides expected a confrontation.

The children were frightened.

The visitors were confused and suspicious.

But the children realised something their game had ignored.

The strangers were people, not decorations for an adventure.

They could talk.

So they did.

The children explained as much as they could.

The visitors told them, in return, that being pulled into somebody else's magical game was not especially enjoyable.

For once, the problem was solved more by conversation than clever tricks.

As sunset approached, the Psammead's magic weakened.

The visitors vanished back to wherever the wish had taken them from.

The children stood quietly in the gravel pit.

Their pretend game suddenly seemed much sillier.

A wish could make an imaginary adventure real.

It could not guarantee that the real people inside it would behave like characters in a game.
