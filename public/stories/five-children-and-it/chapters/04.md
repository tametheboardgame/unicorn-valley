# Wings

After several disasters, the children decided they needed a wish that could not possibly go wrong.

They thought for a long time.

Then Jane said, “What about wings?”

Everyone liked that.

Flying meant freedom.

No shops.

No suspicious adults.

No one trying to carry away the Lamb.

They found the Psammead.

“We wish for wings.”

The sand-fairy puffed itself up.

Feathers burst from four small backs.

<!-- block:into-the-sky -->
The first few moments were chaos.

Robert crashed into a bush.

Jane flew backwards.

Cyril insisted he understood exactly what he was doing while flying sideways into Anthea.

Then they learned.

They rose above the gravel pit.

The fields spread beneath them.

Roads became ribbons.

Houses looked like toys.

For the first time, a wish felt entirely wonderful.

They flew for miles.

<!-- block:too-far-from-home -->
Flying also made distance seem unimportant.

They went farther than they intended.

By lunchtime they were hungry.

They landed near a church and found themselves on the tower.

From that height they could see almost the whole countryside.

They ate what food they had managed to gather and talked about flying somewhere even farther.

Then Anthea looked at the sun.

It was already low.

Everyone remembered the rule at exactly the same moment.

The wings disappeared at sunset.

The children looked over the edge of the tower.

The ground was very far below.

The door leading down was locked.

Cyril tested it again.

Still locked.

The sun touched the horizon.

Feathers vanished.

Four children stood on the church tower with no wings at all.

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

Then Robert said, “We may have found a flaw in the plan.”
