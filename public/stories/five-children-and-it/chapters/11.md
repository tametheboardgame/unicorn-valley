# The Last Wish

The next morning brought wonderful news.

Mother and Father were coming home.

For once the children did not want a wish for themselves.

They wanted something lovely for Mother.

Then Martha arrived with news from a nearby house.

Jewellery had been stolen during the night.

There were necklaces, rings and precious stones missing.

Jane listened.

“Wouldn't it be lovely if Mother found beautiful jewels waiting for her?”

The others turned towards her.

Jane's face changed.

“I wish she would.”

Too late.

<!-- block:the-jewels-appear -->
The children searched Mother's room.

At first they found nothing.

When Mother arrived home, however, magical jewels began appearing.

They were beautiful.

They were also unmistakably somebody else's stolen property.

The children were horrified.

They could not let Mother be blamed.

This wish had to be fixed.

They searched for the Psammead.

For hours they found nothing.

At last the sand-fairy appeared.

It looked exhausted.

The children explained.

They asked for the stolen jewels to be returned.

The Psammead granted the wish.

<!-- block:one-last-favour -->
Then the sand-fairy asked for something unusual.

It wanted a wish for itself.

It was tired of being discovered, dug up and asked to solve human problems.

Anthea understood.

She made one final wish.

The Psammead would be left in peace.

No one would find it again unless it chose to be found.

The creature puffed itself up one last time.

“Done.”

The sand settled.

The gravel pit became quiet.

The children waited.

Nothing moved.

<!-- block:what-they-kept -->
Their wishes had left them with no gold.

No wings.

No castle.

No magical beauty.

Robert was ordinary-sized.

The Lamb was a toddler.

The jewels belonged to their rightful owner.

Yet the children had kept something.

They had stories nobody else would quite believe.

They had learned that wanting something and knowing what will make you happy are not the same thing.

And they had learned to be very careful with the words:

“I wish.”
