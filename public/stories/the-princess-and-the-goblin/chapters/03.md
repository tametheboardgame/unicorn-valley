# Curdie the Miner

A few days later Irene went walking with Lootie.

They went farther than usual.

Clouds covered the sun, and Lootie suddenly realised evening was approaching.

They hurried home.

Then strange voices sounded among the rocks.

Goblins.

Lootie panicked.

Irene heard rough laughter getting closer.

Before the goblins reached them, another voice rang across the hillside.

It was singing.

The goblins stopped.

A boy stepped out of the dusk carrying a miner's lamp.

His name was Curdie.

He knew the mountain paths and the goblins beneath them.

More importantly, he knew something the goblins hated.

Rhymes.

<!-- block:curdies-rhyme -->
Curdie sang silly verses as loudly as he could.

The goblins covered their ears.

They hated songs and rhymes from the people above ground.

Curdie marched Irene and Lootie safely back towards the house while continuing to sing.

Once the danger had passed, Irene thanked him.

Curdie was about twelve and worked in the mines with his father.

He was practical, curious and much less impressed by princesses than Lootie thought proper.

Irene liked him immediately.

Curdie warned Lootie that the goblins had been coming closer to the surface.

Something was changing underground.

Then he headed back towards the miners' cottages.

<!-- block:curdies-curiosity -->
Curdie had been thinking about the goblins too.

He knew their feet were unusually soft and vulnerable.

He knew they came into the mines at night.

And he knew they were becoming bolder.

Curdie decided that simply staying away from them was not enough.

He wanted to know what they were planning.

So while Irene returned to her bright rooms above ground, Curdie began thinking about the dark tunnels underneath the mountain.
