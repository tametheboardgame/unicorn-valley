# King-Papa

Not long afterwards Irene heard a bugle across the mountain.

She knew that sound.

Her father was coming.

Irene ran towards the gate.

The King's riders appeared along the road, armour flashing in the sunlight.

Irene lifted her arms.

Her father leaned from his horse and pulled her up beside him.

For the rest of the day Irene forgot almost everything except that King-Papa was home.

<!-- block:curdies-warning -->
Curdie wanted to warn the King.

The difficulty was explaining how he knew.

He had been creeping through forbidden tunnels, listening to goblins and taking risks adults were unlikely to approve of.

Still, he tried to tell people around the house that the goblins were planning something.

Some listened.

Most thought he was exaggerating.

The mountain had always had goblins.

The house had thick walls.

There were guards.

What could underground creatures possibly do?

Curdie found this extremely frustrating.

<!-- block:irene-remembers -->
Irene told her father about Curdie.

She also tried to tell him about her great-great-grandmother.

The King listened kindly.

He did not laugh at her.

But Irene could tell he was not certain what to believe.

After he left the mountain again, Irene thought about the hidden rooms upstairs.

She wanted to see her grandmother more than ever.

This time, when she climbed the old staircase, she found the right way.

The mysterious room was waiting.

So was the old lady.

And this time she had something important for Irene.
