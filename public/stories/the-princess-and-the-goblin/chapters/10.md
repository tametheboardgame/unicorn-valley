# The Rescue

Irene followed the thread through passages she had never seen.

Curdie followed Irene.

Sometimes the route seemed completely wrong.

At one point the thread led deeper into the mountain.

Curdie objected.

Irene kept going.

Soon they reached a place where goblin voices echoed nearby.

Curdie wanted to choose another tunnel.

Irene refused to leave the thread.

They slipped past the goblins.

<!-- block:back-to-the-surface -->
Eventually fresh air touched their faces.

The thread had brought them out.

Curdie was safe.

But instead of thanking the invisible guide, he still doubted Irene's explanation.

He believed in goblins because he had seen them.

He believed in mines because he worked in them.

An invisible thread connected to a mysterious grandmother was harder.

Irene became angry.

Then sad.

She had expected Curdie to understand.

<!-- block:the-promise -->
Curdie realised he had hurt her.

He apologised for speaking as though she had invented the whole thing.

He still did not understand what had happened.

That, he admitted, was different from knowing Irene was wrong.

It was not a perfect apology.

But it was a beginning.

Curdie returned to his parents with news of the goblin plan.

Irene returned to the King's house.

The goblins were running out of time.

So were the people above them.
