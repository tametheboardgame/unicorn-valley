# The Silver Thread

Irene's great-great-grandmother gave her a ring.

It looked simple enough.

The old lady explained that the ring was connected to a thread.

Not an ordinary thread.

A thread so fine that Irene might not always be able to see it.

“When you are in real trouble,” her grandmother said, “put the ring beneath your pillow. Then follow the thread.”

“Where will it lead?”

“Where you need to go.”

Irene frowned.

“Will it always lead somewhere safe?”

Her grandmother smiled.

“Not necessarily where you think safety is.”

That answer was not especially comforting.

<!-- block:the-thread -->
The old lady showed Irene the shining thread.

It seemed to run from the ring to a tiny silver ball.

For a moment Irene could see it clearly.

Then it vanished.

She could still feel it between her fingers.

Her grandmother told her that sometimes following the thread would require trust.

Irene promised she would remember.

<!-- block:a-strange-test -->
Later Irene tried to explain the ring to Lootie.

Lootie saw only an ordinary ring.

When Irene spoke about an invisible thread, Lootie became worried.

Irene stopped arguing.

She was beginning to understand something.

Seeing a thing herself did not guarantee that everyone else would see it too.

That did not automatically make either person foolish or dishonest.

The thought made Irene calmer.

She kept the ring close.

Far below the house, Curdie was still exploring the goblin tunnels.

Soon both children would need clues.

Only one of those clues could be seen.
