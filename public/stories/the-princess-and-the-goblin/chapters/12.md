# Water Under the Mountain

Deep underground, the goblins broke through the barriers holding back the water.

They expected the flood to rush into the human mines.

Instead, their digging had changed the passages.

Water poured into the goblins' own tunnels.

The underground kingdom filled rapidly.

Goblins fled towards higher ground.

Some escaped.

Others were forced away from the mountain altogether.

Their great plan had turned against them.

<!-- block:after-the-flood -->
The human miners survived because Curdie's warnings had made them watchful.

The King's house was safe.

Princess Irene was safe.

Curdie returned to work, though everyone treated his warnings much more seriously from then on.

The King thanked him.

Irene wanted Curdie rewarded too.

Curdie did not ask for riches.

He preferred to return to his parents and the mountain he knew.

<!-- block:what-irene-kept -->
Irene continued visiting her mysterious great-great-grandmother.

Sometimes the old lady was easy to find.

Sometimes Irene searched and found nothing.

The silver thread did not become less mysterious simply because Irene had followed it successfully.

Curdie had learned something similar.

Not everything true was immediately visible.

Not everything invisible had to be believed without question either.

Sometimes you followed the evidence you had.

Sometimes you trusted someone who had earned that trust.

And sometimes, when the mountain was dark and the path was impossible to see, a thread you could barely feel was enough to take the next step.
