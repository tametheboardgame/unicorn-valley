# The Old Lady Upstairs

Irene stared at the old lady.

“Who are you?”

“I am your great-great-grandmother.”

Irene tried to count how many “greats” that was supposed to mean.

The old lady smiled.

She seemed very old and very young at the same time.

Her room was warm and bright. A strange lamp glowed beside her spinning wheel.

Irene asked why nobody had ever told her about this part of the house.

Her great-great-grandmother said some things could only be found when the time was right.

That was not exactly an answer.

It was, Irene would discover, the sort of answer her grandmother often gave.

The old lady took Irene to another room.

White pigeons fluttered around them.

Their wings filled the air with a soft rushing sound.

Irene clapped her hands.

For a little while she forgot that she had ever been frightened.

Then her grandmother showed her the way back downstairs.

Irene ran to Lootie and told her everything.

Lootie did not believe a word.

“There is no old lady upstairs.”

“There is.”

“There isn't.”

“I spoke to her.”

Lootie insisted Irene had dreamed it.

Irene became upset.

She knew what she had seen.

But when she tried to find the hidden rooms again, she could not.

The staircase was there.

The passages were there.

The right door seemed to have vanished.

That night Irene lay awake thinking about the old lady.

Perhaps Lootie was right.

Perhaps it had been a dream.

Then, faintly from somewhere high above, Irene thought she heard a spinning wheel turning.
