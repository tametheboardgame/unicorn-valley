# Beneath the Mountain

Curdie spent his days working in the mine with his father.

He knew its ordinary tunnels well.

The goblin passages were different.

They twisted away through cracks and hidden openings where human miners rarely went.

One night Curdie followed the sound of goblin voices.

He carried a lamp, a pickaxe and as much courage as he could manage.

The tunnels narrowed.

The air grew colder.

Then he heard footsteps.

Curdie hid.

<!-- block:goblins-in-the-dark -->
A group of goblins passed nearby.

They were ugly, but not in the way Curdie expected.

Some were short and broad.

Some had enormous heads.

Their skin looked pale from generations underground.

They wore strange shoes to protect their tender feet.

Curdie followed at a distance.

The goblins entered a huge underground hall.

There he saw their King and Queen.

Their son, Prince Harelip, was arguing about a plan.

Curdie could not hear everything.

But he heard enough to understand that the goblins wanted revenge against the people above.

Something important was being prepared.

<!-- block:curdie-listens -->
Curdie crept closer.

He learned that the goblins knew passages leading towards the King's house.

He also heard talk of Princess Irene.

That made him forget to be careful.

A stone moved beneath his foot.

The goblins turned.

Curdie ran.

He escaped through passages too narrow for some of the larger goblins and reached the human mine again.

He did not yet know the whole plan.

But he knew one thing.

The danger was not an old story used to frighten children indoors after dark.

The goblins were planning something real.
