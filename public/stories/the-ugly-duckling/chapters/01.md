# The Last Egg

It was summer in the countryside.

The fields were green, the water shone in the sun, and beneath the broad leaves beside an old farmhouse a mother duck sat patiently on her nest.

One by one, the eggs began to crack.

Tap.

Crack.

Peep!

Small yellow ducklings pushed their way into the world.

All except one egg.

It was larger than the others and showed no sign of opening.

The mother duck waited.

And waited.

At last the shell cracked.

Out tumbled a large grey duckling with long legs and rather untidy feathers.

His brothers and sisters stared at him.

“He’s very big,” said one.

“He’s very grey,” said another.

Their mother looked at him carefully.

“He swims well,” she said. “That seems a good start.”

<!-- block:into-the-yard -->
The next day she led all the ducklings into the farmyard.

The grey duckling paddled beautifully.

He followed directions.

He tried to be polite.

But the other birds noticed immediately that he looked different.

Some laughed.

Some pecked at him.

A turkey puffed himself up and chased him around a water trough.

“Why do you look like that?” another duck asked.

The grey duckling had no answer.

He had only just hatched.

He did not know why he looked like anything.

<!-- block:trying-to-fit -->
Day after day he tried harder.

He walked where the other ducklings walked.

He ate what they ate.

He copied the way they tucked their heads beneath their wings.

But somehow he was always too tall, too grey, too awkward or simply too different.

At night he would curl up near the edge of the nest.

“Perhaps tomorrow will be easier,” he would think.

But tomorrow never was.

One morning, after being chased from the feeding bowl again, the young duckling looked beyond the fence towards the open fields.

He was frightened of leaving.

He was also frightened that if he stayed, he might eventually believe everything the other birds said about him.

So he squeezed through a gap in the hedge and ran.
