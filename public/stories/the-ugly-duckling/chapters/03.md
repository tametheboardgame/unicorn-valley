# The Little House

An old woman lived in the house with a cat and a hen.

The next morning she discovered the duckling.

“Oh!” she said. “Perhaps you are a duck who can lay eggs.”

The duckling did not know how to explain that he could not.

Still, the house was warm.

For a while he stayed.

The hen was very proud of laying eggs.

The cat was very proud of purring.

They could not understand why the duckling wanted anything else.

<!-- block:dreaming-of-water -->
One morning the duckling looked through the open door.

Sunlight glittered on a distant pond.

“I would like to swim,” he said.

“Why?” asked the hen.

“Because I love the feeling of water around me.”

The hen stared at him.

“That sounds extremely inconvenient.”

The cat agreed.

“You should learn to purr.”

“I can’t purr.”

“Then lay an egg,” said the hen.

“I can’t do that either.”

The duckling sighed.

The little house was safer than the farmyard.

But being safe was not quite the same thing as belonging.

<!-- block:leaving-again -->
So he thanked the old woman for sheltering him and left.

Outside, the world was changing.

Summer had faded.

Leaves turned yellow and red.

The air smelled colder.

The duckling found ponds and streams and quiet places among the reeds.

He was lonely.

But he could swim whenever he liked.

He could stretch his wings.

He could watch the sky.

For the first time, he began to learn what he enjoyed when nobody else was telling him what he ought to be.
