# Away from the Farm

The duckling ran until the farmyard sounds disappeared behind him.

He crossed a field.

He pushed through tall grass.

At last he reached a wide marsh where wild ducks lived among the reeds.

They looked at him curiously.

“You’re an odd-looking duck,” one said.

The young bird lowered his head.

“But you can stay here if you don’t bother anyone,” said another.

It was not exactly a welcome.

But it was better than being chased.

For two days he rested among the reeds.

<!-- block:the-marsh -->
Then hunters came to the marsh.

There were loud cracks in the distance.

Birds burst into the air.

The duckling flattened himself beneath the reeds and did not move.

A huge hunting dog pushed through the grass.

It stopped directly in front of him.

The duckling squeezed his eyes shut.

The dog sniffed.

Then it turned and ran away.

The duckling opened one eye.

“Well,” he whispered, “apparently even that dog wasn’t interested in me.”

He waited until everything was quiet before moving again.

<!-- block:through-the-storm -->
Wind swept across the fields that evening.

Rain fell sideways.

The duckling struggled on through mud and wet grass.

He no longer knew where he was going.

He only knew he needed shelter.

Just before dark he saw a crooked little house.

One corner leaned one way.

The roof leaned another.

A gap beneath the door was just big enough for a tired young bird.

The duckling squeezed through and collapsed inside.
