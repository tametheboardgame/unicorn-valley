# Spring on the Lake

Nothing pecked him.

Nothing laughed.

One swan simply said, “Hello.”

The duckling looked up.

The swans were beside him now.

They looked calm.

Curious.

Friendly.

He stared down into the water.

A white bird stared back.

The duckling twisted his neck.

The reflection did the same.

He stretched one wing.

A broad white wing opened across the water.

He was not looking at a duckling at all.

He was looking at himself.

A young swan.

<!-- block:what-had-changed -->
For a moment he could not speak.

All those months he had believed there was something wrong with him because he had failed at being like the ducks around him.

But he had never been meant to grow into the same shape.

He had not become worthwhile because his feathers were white now.

He had been worthwhile when they were grey.

The difference was that, at last, he could see where he belonged.

<!-- block:a-new-season -->
Children came to the lake and threw crumbs onto the water.

“There’s a new swan!” one called.

The young swan felt shy.

Then one of the older swans nudged him gently.

“Come on,” she said. “You’ll get used to being noticed.”

The young swan laughed.

He lifted his wings.

Spring sunlight shone across the lake.

He remembered the frightened grey bird squeezing through the farmyard hedge.

He wished he could tell him something.

Keep going.

The world is bigger than this yard.

So he swam out across the bright water with the other swans.

Not because he had finally become good enough to belong.

Because he had finally reached a place where he did.
