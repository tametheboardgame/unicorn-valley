# Winter

Then winter arrived.

The pond began to freeze.

Each night the circle of open water around the duckling grew smaller.

He paddled constantly to stop the ice closing around him.

One morning he was too tired.

The water froze.

The duckling became trapped.

A farmer walking across the field spotted him, broke the ice and carried him home.

<!-- block:indoors -->
The farmer’s children were excited to see him.

They tried to bring him food.

They tried to stroke him.

But the duckling had spent so long expecting to be chased that he panicked.

He flapped across the room.

A bowl fell over.

A chair tipped sideways.

Someone shouted.

The duckling found an open door and fled back into the snow.

It was not the children’s fault.

They had meant to help.

But fear does not always stop to ask what someone meant.

<!-- block:long-cold-days -->
The rest of the winter was hard.

The duckling sheltered beneath bushes and beside frozen streams.

He ate whatever he could find.

Some days seemed to last forever.

But eventually the light changed.

Snow began dripping from branches.

Small green shoots appeared through the earth.

The duckling had survived.

And without quite noticing, he had grown.
