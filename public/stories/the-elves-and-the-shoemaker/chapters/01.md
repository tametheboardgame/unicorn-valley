# The Elves and the Shoemaker

Once there was a shoemaker who worked carefully and honestly, but business had been very quiet.

One winter evening he looked around his little workshop and found that he had only enough leather left to make one pair of shoes.

He cut the leather into all the right shapes and laid the pieces neatly on his workbench.

“I’ll stitch them first thing in the morning,” he told his wife.

Then they put out the lamp and went to bed.

<!-- block:mysterious-help -->
When the shoemaker came downstairs the next morning, he stopped in the doorway.

A finished pair of shoes stood on the bench.

They were beautiful.

Every stitch was straight. Every edge was smooth. The laces were tied in two perfect bows.

The shoemaker turned them over in his hands.

“I certainly didn’t make these,” he said.

His wife smiled. “Then someone very clever did.”

That morning a customer came into the shop, tried on the shoes and loved them so much that she paid a generous price.

With the money, the shoemaker bought enough leather for two more pairs.

That evening he cut out the pieces and left them on the bench.

And the next morning, both pairs were finished.

The same thing happened again.

Two pairs became four. Four became eight.

Every night the shoemaker prepared the leather.

Every morning wonderful new shoes appeared.

Before long, the little shop was busy again.

<!-- block:midnight-secret -->
A few nights before Christmas, the shoemaker said, “Whoever is helping us has been very kind. I think we should find out who they are.”

So he and his wife stayed awake.

They hid quietly behind a curtain where they could see the workbench.

The clock ticked towards midnight.

Then, just as the last bell chimed, two tiny elves slipped into the workshop.

They were no taller than the shoemaker’s boots.

They climbed onto the bench, picked up the leather and began to work.

Their little hands moved incredibly fast.

Snip, stitch, tap.

Snip, stitch, tap.

The shoemaker and his wife could hardly believe their eyes.

Before long, the shoes were finished.

The elves gave each other a cheerful little bow and disappeared into the night.

<!-- block:thank-you-gifts -->
The next morning the shoemaker’s wife said, “Those little elves have helped us when we needed it most. We should do something kind for them.”

“I know exactly what I can make,” said the shoemaker.

He made two tiny pairs of polished shoes.

His wife sewed two tiny shirts, two warm coats and two pairs of trousers.

She even knitted little socks.

On Christmas Eve they placed all the clothes on the workbench instead of leather.

Then they hid behind the curtain once more.

At midnight the elves arrived.

They hurried towards the bench, ready for work, but stopped when they saw the presents.

For a moment they simply stared.

Then they cheered.

They pulled on the shirts and trousers, buttoned their coats and stamped happily in their new shoes.

One elf twirled around the table.

The other leapt onto a chair and danced.

“We’re dressed so smart from head to toe!
There’s no more cobbling left to do!”

They skipped around the workshop, laughing and dancing, then ran out through the door and into the snowy night.

<!-- block:a-kindness-returned -->
The shoemaker never saw the elves again.

But his shop continued to do well, and he never forgot the mysterious little helpers who had worked beside him when times were hard.

And whenever someone in the town needed a little help, the shoemaker remembered what the elves had done for him.

Whenever he could, he helped them too.

Because kindness, once given, has a funny way of finding its way back into the world.
