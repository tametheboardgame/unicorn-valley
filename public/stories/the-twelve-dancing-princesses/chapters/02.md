# The Curious Traveller

One afternoon a traveller arrived at the palace.

She had spent years walking from place to place, fixing fences, helping at farms, carrying messages and taking whatever honest work she could find.

When she heard about the king’s mystery, she was curious.

“I don’t especially need a royal reward,” she said, “but I would very much like to know what wears out twelve pairs of shoes in one night.”

On her way to the palace she met an old woman resting beside the road.

The traveller shared her bread and cheese with her.

When the old woman heard where she was going, she smiled.

“Then you’ll need two pieces of advice,” she said.

“First, if someone offers you a bedtime drink, do not swallow it.”

She reached into her basket and pulled out a soft grey cloak.

“Second, wear this after midnight.”

“What does it do?” asked the traveller.

“Try it.”

The traveller put on the cloak.

Her hands vanished.

Her boots vanished.

Even her shadow disappeared.

That evening the traveller was given a room beside the princesses.

The eldest princess brought her a warm cup scented with berries and spices.

“For a good night’s sleep,” she said pleasantly.

The traveller thanked her.

But when the princess had gone, she poured the drink into a flowerpot and climbed into bed.

Soon she heard whispering through the wall.

“Is she asleep?”

“Listen.”

The traveller began to snore.

The princesses giggled.

Then came the sound of cupboards opening, fabric rustling and buckles clicking.

The traveller slipped from her bed, wrapped herself in the grey cloak and quietly opened the door.

All twelve princesses were dressed for a ball.

Their oldest sister walked to the first bed and stamped her foot.

The bed sank slowly into the floor.

Beneath it was a staircase.

One by one, the princesses disappeared down the steps.

The invisible traveller followed.
