# The Midnight Ballroom

The boats glided across the underground lake.

On the opposite shore stood a magnificent castle.

Every window blazed with warm light.

Music floated across the water.

As soon as the princesses stepped ashore, the great doors opened.

Inside was the grandest ballroom the traveller had ever seen.

Hundreds of lanterns hung from the ceiling.

Musicians played on a balcony.

The polished floor reflected the dancers like still water.

And the twelve princesses ran straight onto the dance floor.

<!-- block:the-dance -->
They danced in circles.

They danced in pairs.

They danced so quickly that their skirts flew out around them.

The traveller watched from the edge of the room, still invisible.

Now she understood the shoes.

The princesses did not stop.

Whenever one dance ended, another began.

They laughed and spun until midnight became one o’clock, then two, then nearly three.

The traveller even joined in once, dancing by herself where nobody could see her.

She had to cover her mouth to stop herself laughing.

At last the youngest princess collapsed into a chair.

“My feet hurt,” she said.

“That,” said her eldest sister, “is because you insist on jumping during every fast song.”

<!-- block:the-golden-cup -->
Before they left, the traveller took one small golden cup from an empty table.

Not because she wanted to keep it.

She wanted proof.

The princesses crossed the lake again, hurried through the three shining forests and climbed the hidden staircase.

By the time the traveller returned to her own room, the sisters were already changing into their nightclothes.

She slipped into bed and began snoring again.

A few moments later the eldest princess opened the door and looked inside.

The traveller did not move.

The princess quietly closed it.

Only then did the traveller allow herself to smile.
