# The Secret Revealed

The traveller followed the princesses for three nights.

Each time she saw the hidden staircase.

Each time she crossed the silver, golden and diamond forests.

Each time she sailed across the underground lake.

And each time the twelve sisters danced until their shoes were worn through.

On the third morning, the king called the traveller to the throne room.

“Well?” he asked.

“Have you discovered my daughters’ secret?”

“I have,” she said.

The twelve princesses stood nearby.

The youngest looked worried.

The eldest folded her arms.

<!-- block:the-evidence -->
The traveller placed three branches on the table.

Silver.

Gold.

Diamond-bright.

Then she placed the little golden cup beside them.

The princesses stared.

The eldest princess sighed.

“You followed us.”

“I did.”

The king looked from the traveller to his daughters.

“Is it true?”

For a moment nobody spoke.

Then the youngest princess nodded.

“Yes.”

Soon all twelve were talking at once.

They told their father about the staircase, the forests, the lake and the midnight castle.

They told him how they had discovered it by accident.

They told him that they had kept it secret because they were certain he would forbid them from ever returning.

<!-- block:a-better-answer -->
The king was silent for a long time.

At last he said, “I wish you had trusted me.”

The eldest princess looked down.

“We should have.”

“And,” the king added, “you have ruined an astonishing number of shoes.”

That made the youngest princess laugh.

Soon everyone else did too.

The king kept his promise and gave the traveller a generous reward.

But instead of sealing the hidden staircase forever, he made a new rule.

The princesses could visit the midnight ballroom on special nights, provided they told someone where they were going and returned before breakfast.

The royal shoemaker was also given a very large order for stronger dancing shoes.

<!-- block:the-next-dance -->
As for the traveller, she stayed at the palace for a while.

The princesses liked her.

She knew how to keep a secret when it mattered, and how to tell the truth when it mattered more.

One evening the youngest princess found her in the garden.

“We’re going dancing tonight,” she whispered.

The traveller raised an eyebrow.

“Does your father know?”

The princess grinned.

“He does.”

“Then,” said the traveller, “I hope you ordered me a pair of dancing shoes too.”

And that night, beneath the palace, thirteen pairs of shoes danced beneath the lanterns.
