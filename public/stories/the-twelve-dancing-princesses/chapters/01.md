# The Worn-Out Shoes

There was once a king who lived in a great palace with his twelve daughters.

The princesses were very different from one another. Some were loud, some quiet. Some loved books, some loved horses, some could spend an entire afternoon arguing about ribbons.

But there was one thing they all agreed on.

They loved to dance.

Every evening the twelve sisters went to their shared room at the top of the palace. Their father wished them goodnight, the doors were closed, and the palace became quiet.

Yet every morning something very strange had happened.

Their dancing shoes were worn right through.

Not a little scuffed.

Not slightly dusty.

Completely worn out, as if every princess had spent the whole night dancing.

<!-- block:no-one-knew -->
The king asked the guards.

They had seen nothing.

He asked the servants.

They had heard nothing.

He even inspected the windows himself. They were far too high above the gardens for anyone to climb down.

“Perhaps your shoes are simply very badly made,” said the king.

The royal shoemaker looked horrified.

So the next night the king placed twelve brand-new pairs of shoes beside the princesses’ beds.

In the morning every pair had holes in the soles.

The eldest princess only smiled.

The youngest tried very hard not to.

The king knew immediately that his daughters were keeping a secret.

<!-- block:a-reward-offered -->
He announced that anyone who could discover where the princesses went at night would receive a generous royal reward.

Many clever people tried.

They listened outside doors.

They watched from corridors.

They counted footsteps.

But somehow, every watcher fell asleep before midnight.

And every morning the princesses’ shoes were worn through again.

Soon the mystery of the twelve dancing princesses was being talked about in every town and village in the kingdom.
