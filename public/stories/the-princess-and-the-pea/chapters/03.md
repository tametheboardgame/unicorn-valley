# The Tiny Pea

The next morning everyone gathered for breakfast.

The visitor came downstairs looking exhausted.

The queen raised an eyebrow.

“Did you sleep well?”

“Not really,” said the young woman.

“Oh?”

“I’m terribly sorry to complain, but there seemed to be something hard in the bed.”

The queen leaned forward.

“Something hard?”

“I hardly slept at all. I feel as though I have bruises everywhere.”

The prince stared at the queen.

The queen looked delighted.

She explained about the pea beneath the enormous tower of mattresses.

The visitor blinked.

“You put a pea under my bed?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“To see whether you would notice.”

There was a long silence.

Then the visitor started laughing.

<!-- block:not-the-test-expected -->
The prince laughed too.

Even the queen eventually smiled.

“Well,” said the visitor, “I suppose I passed your test.”

“You did,” said the queen.

“But I think I would prefer a normal bed tonight.”

“That,” said the prince, “seems entirely reasonable.”

The prince and the visiting princess spent the next few days talking.

They discovered that they both loved long walks, terrible jokes and breakfast far more than formal banquets.

In time, they decided that they wanted to marry.

Not because of the pea.

Not because of the mattresses.

Because they actually liked one another.

<!-- block:the-famous-pea -->
The tiny pea was kept in the palace museum.

Visitors came from miles around to see it.

Some believed the story.

Some did not.

And the princess never minded either way.

Whenever anyone asked whether the pea really proved she was a princess, she would smile.

“No,” she said.

“It mostly proved that the queen owned far too many mattresses.”
