# A Storm at the Palace

Once there was a prince who wanted to marry a princess.

Not just someone wearing a crown.

Not someone who had memorised all the correct royal bows.

He wanted to meet someone who was truly herself.

So he travelled from kingdom to kingdom.

He met princesses who could ride beautifully.

Princesses who could sing.

Princesses who could name every fork at a banquet.

But every time he returned home, he felt that something had not quite clicked.

His mother, the queen, began to wonder whether he would ever make up his mind.

Then one night a tremendous storm rolled over the palace.

Thunder shook the windows.

Rain hammered against the roof.

Wind howled down the chimneys.

Suddenly there was a knock at the palace gate.

A guard opened the door.

On the step stood a young woman.

Her hair was soaked.

Water dripped from her sleeves.

Mud covered the hem of her dress.

“I’m sorry to arrive like this,” she said, “but the storm caught me on the road.”

The queen came to see who had arrived.

The young woman gave a small bow.

“I am a princess,” she said.

The queen looked at the rainwater pooling around her boots.

“A princess?”

“Yes.”

The queen did not say whether she believed her.

Instead, she smiled.

“Then you must stay here tonight.”
