# Advice from a Caterpillar

The Caterpillar stared at Alice.

“Who are you?” it asked.

Alice hesitated.

“I hardly know. I have changed size so many times today that I don't feel quite like the same person.”

The Caterpillar did not seem impressed.

It asked Alice to explain herself more clearly.

Alice tried.

The Caterpillar asked awkward questions.

Alice became annoyed.

At last the Caterpillar climbed down from the mushroom.

“One side will make you grow taller,” it said, “and the other side will make you grow shorter.”

“One side of what?”

“The mushroom.”

Then it crawled away.

<!-- block:mushroom-pieces -->
Alice broke off a piece from each side.

She tested one.

Her neck stretched high above the trees.

A Pigeon immediately accused her of being a serpent because her long neck was near its nest.

Alice insisted that she was a little girl.

The Pigeon remained doubtful.

Alice tried the other piece of mushroom.

After several alarming changes, she finally managed to become roughly her usual size.

“That,” she said, “is much better.”

Ahead of her stood a small house.

Alice decided that, in Wonderland, being the correct size before knocking on a stranger's door was probably sensible.
