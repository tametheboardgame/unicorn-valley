# Down the Rabbit-Hole

Alice was beginning to get very tired of sitting beside her sister on the riverbank.

Her sister had a book, but it seemed to contain no pictures and no conversations.

Alice could not understand the point of a book like that.

She was considering whether making a daisy chain was worth the trouble of getting up when something unusual ran past.

<!-- block:a-rabbit-with-a-watch -->
It was a White Rabbit with pink eyes.

That alone was surprising.

More surprising was hearing him say, “Oh dear! Oh dear! I shall be too late!”

Then the Rabbit pulled a watch from his waistcoat pocket.

Alice jumped to her feet.

She had never before seen a rabbit with a waistcoat pocket, or a watch to take out of it.

She ran after him.

The Rabbit disappeared down a large rabbit-hole beneath a hedge.

Alice followed without stopping to wonder how she would get back.

<!-- block:the-long-fall -->
The tunnel dropped sharply.

Alice fell.

And fell.

And kept falling.

She passed cupboards and shelves built into the walls. She saw maps, pictures and jars.

She wondered how far down she had travelled.

Perhaps she was falling right through the middle of the earth.

At last she landed safely on a heap of dry leaves.

Ahead of her, the White Rabbit hurried around a corner.

Alice ran after him and found herself in a long hall filled with locked doors.

On a glass table lay a tiny golden key.

It fitted a very small door.

Through that door Alice could see the loveliest garden she had ever seen.

There was only one problem.

She was much too big to fit through.
