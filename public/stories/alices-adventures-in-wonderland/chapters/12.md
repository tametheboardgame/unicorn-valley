# Alice's Evidence

Alice jumped up so quickly that she knocked over the jury box.

Tiny jurors tumbled everywhere.

She carefully picked them up and put them back.

The King told her about Rule Forty-Two.

“All persons more than a mile high must leave the court.”

“I am not a mile high,” Alice said.

“You are,” said the King.

“Nearly two miles,” said the Queen.

Alice folded her arms.

“I won't leave.”

The court continued.

A mysterious poem was produced as evidence.

Nobody could explain what it proved.

The King tried anyway.

Alice listened as long as she could.

Then she had had enough.

<!-- block:only-a-pack-of-cards -->
“There is no sense in this trial,” she said.

The Queen jumped to her feet.

“Off with her head!”

Alice looked around the court.

The King.

The Queen.

The soldiers.

The jurors.

Suddenly they no longer seemed frightening.

“You're nothing but a pack of cards!”

The whole court rose into the air.

Cards flew towards her in a storm.

Alice cried out and tried to brush them away.

<!-- block:waking-up -->
She opened her eyes.

She was lying on the riverbank.

Her sister was beside her.

A few leaves had fluttered onto Alice's face.

There was no Queen.

No Caterpillar.

No talking Rabbit.

No endless tea party.

Alice sat up.

“What a curious dream I've had!”

She told her sister as much as she could remember.

Then Alice ran home for tea.

Behind her, the river moved quietly through the afternoon.

For a moment the ordinary world felt almost surprising.

After Wonderland, perhaps it always would.
