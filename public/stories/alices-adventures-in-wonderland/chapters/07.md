# A Mad Tea-Party

Alice found the March Hare's house.

A long table stood outside beneath a tree.

The March Hare and the Hatter were having tea with a sleepy Dormouse between them.

“No room!” they shouted when Alice approached.

“There is plenty of room,” said Alice, sitting down.

The tea party turned out to have unusual rules.

The Hatter asked riddles without knowing the answers.

The March Hare offered Alice wine even though there was none.

The Dormouse fell asleep during his own stories.

Nobody seemed interested in being sensible.

Alice learned that the Hatter had quarrelled with Time.

Since then, it had always been six o'clock at the tea table.

Tea time forever.

That was why they kept moving around the table whenever the cups became dirty.

Alice thought this sounded exhausting.

The Hatter told her she should not make personal remarks.

Alice pointed out that everyone at the table had been making personal remarks about her from the moment she arrived.

The conversation became even more ridiculous.

At last Alice stood up.

“This is the stupidest tea party I have ever been to.”

She walked away.

Nobody tried very hard to stop her.

In a tree nearby Alice noticed a door.

She opened it.

Beyond was the long hall she had seen at the beginning of her adventure.

This time she knew exactly what to do.

She took the golden key, adjusted her size with the mushroom and stepped through the little door.

At last she entered the beautiful garden.
