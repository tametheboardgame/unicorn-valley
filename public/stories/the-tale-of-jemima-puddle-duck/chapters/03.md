# Nine Eggs

Jemima returned to the wood every afternoon.

Soon there were nine large greenish-white eggs in the nest.

She was delighted.

The sandy-whiskered gentleman seemed delighted too.

Perhaps slightly too delighted.

Whenever Jemima left, he counted the eggs carefully.

One.

Two.

Three.

All the way to nine.

<!-- block:a-dinner-invitation -->
At last Jemima announced that she would begin sitting on the nest the following day.

“I’ll bring some corn,” she said, “so I hardly need to leave the eggs at all.”

The gentleman waved one paw.

“No need. I shall provide everything.”

Then he made another suggestion.

“Before you settle in, perhaps we should have a little dinner.”

Jemima thought that sounded very friendly.

“Would you bring some sage, thyme, mint, parsley and two onions from the farm?”

Jemima blinked.

“That is an unusual shopping list.”

“For the dinner,” said the gentleman quickly.

Jemima nodded.

On the way home she repeated the list so she would not forget.

Sage.

Thyme.

Mint.

Parsley.

Two onions.

She did not yet realise that those were rather worrying ingredients for a fox to request from a duck.
