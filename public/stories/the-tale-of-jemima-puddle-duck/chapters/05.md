# Home to the Farm

When Jemima reached the clearing, the sandy-whiskered gentleman was waiting.

He was no longer reading his newspaper.

He kept looking between the trees.

“You are late,” he said.

Jemima stopped.

He had never sounded impatient before.

“Bring the herbs inside.”

Jemima suddenly noticed the feathers again.

All those feathers.

She thought about Kep's questions.

Then she thought about the gentleman's tail.

For the first time, the truth began to fit together.

Jemima took one careful step backwards.

<!-- block:back-to-safety -->
A bark sounded from the trees.

Then another.

Kep burst into the clearing with two other dogs close behind him.

The sandy-whiskered gentleman spun around and fled into the wood.

The dogs chased him away.

Kep hurried to Jemima.

“Are you all right?”

Jemima nodded.

Then she rushed to check the nest.

In all the confusion the eggs had been damaged.

Jemima was heartbroken.

Kep stayed beside her until she was ready to leave.

They walked back to the farm together.

<!-- block:a-second-chance -->
Later that summer Jemima made another nest.

This time she chose a safe place close to home.

And this time everyone agreed to let her try sitting on the eggs herself.

Four ducklings hatched.

Jemima was very proud of them.

She still liked adventures.

She still liked making her own decisions.

But she had learned something useful.

Someone can sound polite and still be dangerous.

And when a sensible dog asks why a fox wants you to bring onions to dinner, it is probably worth listening.
