# Jemima's Plan

Jemima Puddle-Duck lived on a busy farm.

She was a handsome white duck with a blue bonnet, a patterned shawl and a very determined way of waddling when she had made up her mind.

At the moment, Jemima had made up her mind about eggs.

She wanted to hatch her own.

Unfortunately, whenever Jemima chose a nesting place around the farm, someone found it.

The farmer's wife worried that Jemima would wander off and leave the eggs cold.

Jemima thought this was extremely unfair.

“They are my eggs,” she quacked. “I should be allowed to sit on them myself.”

<!-- block:a-quiet-nest -->
So Jemima decided to find a secret nest.

Not beside the barn.

Not behind the haystack.

Not anywhere near the farmhouse.

She would go somewhere nobody else knew about.

One fine spring afternoon she set off along the cart road.

At the top of the hill she saw a wood.

“That,” said Jemima, “looks quiet.”

She ran downhill, flapped hard and managed to get herself into the air.

Jemima did not fly often.

Her first few wingbeats were rather untidy.

But once she was moving, she skimmed over the treetops surprisingly well.

Below her she spotted a clearing.

Perfect.

Jemima landed and began looking for the safest, softest, most private nesting place she could find.
