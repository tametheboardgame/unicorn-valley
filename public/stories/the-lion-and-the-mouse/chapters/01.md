# The Lion and the Mouse

At the hottest part of the afternoon, a lion stretched beneath an acacia tree and fell asleep. His paws were broad, his mane was warm with sunlight, and even the birds kept their songs quiet around him.

A little mouse hurried through the grass with a seed tucked between her teeth. She was watching the sky for hawks instead of the ground beneath her paws, so she scampered straight across the lion's tail.

The lion woke with a roar. One great paw came down in front of the mouse, another behind her, and suddenly the whole world seemed to be made of lion.

<!-- block:mouse-promise -->
"Please let me go," squeaked the mouse. "I know I am small, but one day I might be able to help you."

The lion blinked. The thought of such a tiny creature helping him seemed very funny indeed. Still, the mouse had been brave enough to make a promise while standing between his paws. The lion lifted one paw and let her run free.

"Go on then," he said. "I will remember your enormous promise."

The mouse bowed so low that her whiskers brushed the dust. "And I will remember your kindness."

<!-- block:lion-net -->
A few days later, the mouse heard a roar rolling through the trees. This time it did not sound proud. It sounded frightened.

She followed the sound and found the lion tangled in a hunter's rope net. The harder he pulled, the tighter the knots became.

The mouse did not try to pull the ropes. She did not try to roar louder than the lion. She simply began to chew.

One strand snapped, then another. Her teeth were small, but they were exactly the right size for the work. Before long, a gap opened wide enough for the lion to push through.

<!-- block:kindness-returns -->
The lion shook the loose ropes from his mane and stared at the mouse.

"I laughed when you said you might help me," he admitted.

The mouse smiled. "You were thinking about how big I was. I was thinking about what I could do."

From that day on, the lion never measured a friend by the size of their paws, and the mouse never forgot that a kindness can travel a long way before it finds its way home.
