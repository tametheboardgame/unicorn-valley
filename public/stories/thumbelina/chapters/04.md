# The Field Mouse and the Mole

The mole liked Thumbelina’s singing.

He began visiting often.

He talked about tunnels.

He talked about grain.

He talked about how inconvenient birds were.

Most of all, he talked about how much better life was underground.

“No rain,” he said.

“No wind.”

“No bright sunlight shining in your eyes.”

Thumbelina missed bright sunlight very much.

But the field mouse thought the mole was excellent.

“What a comfortable life you could have,” she said.

Thumbelina did not answer.

<!-- block:the-cold-bird -->
One day the mole led them through a new tunnel.

A patch of earth had fallen away from the roof.

Above it lay a swallow.

The bird was still and cold.

“Birds,” said the mole. “Always flying about instead of building proper tunnels.”

He walked on.

Thumbelina stayed behind for a moment.

She remembered the birds that had sung above her in summer.

That night, when everyone was asleep, she crept back.

She placed dry grass around the swallow.

Then she laid her own little blanket over his feathers.

“I’m sorry you never reached the warm countries,” she whispered.

A faint sound answered her.

Tap.

Tap.

Thumbelina froze.

The swallow’s heart was still beating.

<!-- block:a-difficult-choice -->
All winter Thumbelina secretly cared for him.

She brought water in a curled leaf.

She packed warm moss around him.

Slowly, the swallow grew stronger.

By spring he could open his wings again.

“I’m flying south,” he told her. “Come with me.”

Thumbelina looked towards the field mouse’s burrow.

The field mouse had sheltered her when she was desperate.

Thumbelina felt she could not simply disappear.

“I can’t,” she said sadly.

The swallow touched his beak gently to her hand.

“Then thank you for saving me.”

He flew up through the opening and vanished into the blue sky.

Thumbelina watched until he became a tiny dot.

Then spring turned to summer.

And the mole asked Thumbelina to marry him.
