# The Beetle and the Seasons

Summer passed.

Thumbelina lived among the flowers and tall grass.

She drank dew from leaves.

She slept beneath clover.

She made herself a little shelter from woven stems.

The birds sang above her.

The sun warmed the ground.

For a while, living alone did not seem so bad.

She could go wherever she liked.

Nobody could decide things for her.

Nobody could carry her away.

Then autumn came.

The flowers folded.

The birds began leaving.

Cold rain flattened the grass.

Thumbelina wrapped herself in dry leaves, but every night became colder.

<!-- block:winter-arrives -->
Soon snow covered the fields.

To Thumbelina, each snowflake seemed as large as a blanket.

She walked for hours through frozen grass until she saw a tiny doorway beneath a pile of corn stalks.

Inside lived an old field mouse.

The field mouse stared at Thumbelina.

“You look frozen.”

“I am,” Thumbelina admitted.

“Can you tell stories?”

“Yes.”

“Can you help sweep?”

“Yes.”

“Then come in before you turn into an icicle.”

Thumbelina stepped into the warm little burrow.

For the first time in weeks, she slept somewhere dry.

<!-- block:a-safe-place -->
The field mouse was kind in a busy, practical sort of way.

She gave Thumbelina food.

Thumbelina helped tidy the storeroom.

In the evenings Thumbelina told stories about flowers, rivers and summer sunlight.

“You have had a very untidy life,” the field mouse said.

Thumbelina could not disagree.

One afternoon the field mouse announced that an important neighbour was coming to visit.

“He owns miles of tunnels,” she said. “He has enormous stores of grain. And he is very sensible.”

A mole arrived wearing a dark velvet coat.

He was polite.

He was rich.

He was also extremely proud of the fact that he never needed to see the sun.
