# The Swallow

The field mouse was delighted.

“The mole is sensible, wealthy and respectable,” she said.

Thumbelina tried to imagine spending every day underground.

No flowers.

No sky.

No birdsong.

The thought made her chest feel tight.

“I’m grateful to him,” Thumbelina said. “But I don’t want to marry him.”

The field mouse frowned.

“You’ll change your mind.”

Preparations began anyway.

Thumbelina was measured for new clothes.

Rooms were cleared in the mole’s tunnels.

Everyone talked about the wedding as though her answer did not matter.

It reminded Thumbelina of the toads beside the river.

She had been carried away once.

She would not let her life be carried away again.

<!-- block:goodbye-to-the-sun -->
On the day before the wedding, Thumbelina climbed outside.

Autumn had returned.

She stood among the stubble and looked up at the enormous sky.

“Goodbye, sun,” she whispered. “I don’t think I shall see you after tomorrow.”

A familiar chirp sounded overhead.

Thumbelina looked up.

The swallow dropped from the sky and landed beside her.

“You!”

“I was passing this way,” he said. “And I hoped I might find you.”

Thumbelina told him everything.

When she finished, the swallow lowered one wing.

“Then come with me now.”

Thumbelina hesitated only for a moment.

She loved the field mouse.

She was grateful for the warm burrow.

But kindness did not mean giving someone else ownership of her future.

She climbed onto the swallow’s back.

“Yes,” she said. “I’m coming.”
