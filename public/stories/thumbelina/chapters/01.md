# The Girl in the Flower

Once there was a woman who wished very much for a child.

One day she was given a single barley seed and told to plant it carefully.

She placed it in a flowerpot beside the window.

Almost at once a green shoot appeared.

It grew taller and taller until a great flower opened, shaped a little like a tulip.

The woman bent down to look inside.

<!-- block:the-flower-opens -->
Sitting in the middle of the flower was a tiny girl.

She was no taller than a thumb.

The woman stared.

The tiny girl stared back.

Then they both smiled.

“I think I shall call you Thumbelina,” said the woman.

Thumbelina’s bed was a polished walnut shell.

A violet petal made a soft mattress.

A rose petal made a blanket.

During the day she sailed across a bowl of water on a tulip leaf, using two horsehairs as oars.

At night she slept beside the window while moonlight shone through the glass.

Thumbelina was tiny, but her world felt safe and enormous.

She knew every corner of the table.

Every flowerpot was a garden.

Every book was a wall.

Every teacup was almost a swimming pool.

She was happy there.

But one warm night, while the window stood slightly open, something climbed into the room.

A large toad hopped onto the table.

She saw Thumbelina asleep beneath her rose-petal blanket.

“What a perfect little bride for my son,” the toad whispered.

Before anyone could wake, she picked up the walnut-shell bed and carried Thumbelina away.

When Thumbelina opened her eyes the next morning, she was no longer beside the familiar window.

She was sitting on a broad green leaf in the middle of a river.

Water rushed past on every side.

And on the bank, two toads were discussing a wedding that Thumbelina had never agreed to.
