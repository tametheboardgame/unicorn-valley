# The River Escape

Thumbelina looked at the river.

She looked at the toads.

Then she shook her head.

“I am not marrying anyone just because somebody else decided it,” she said.

The toads did not listen.

They began preparing a muddy home beneath the riverbank.

But the little fish below the leaf had heard everything.

They thought Thumbelina looked frightened.

So they swam beneath the stalk that held her leaf in place.

Nibble.

Nibble.

Snap.

The stalk broke.

The current caught the leaf and carried Thumbelina away.

At first she was terrified.

The bank rushed past.

Tall reeds leaned above her like trees.

Dragonflies flashed over the water.

But the farther she travelled from the toads, the lighter she felt.

“I don’t know where I’m going,” she said, “but at least I chose to go.”

A white butterfly fluttered beside the leaf.

Thumbelina loosened the ribbon from around her waist and gently looped it around the butterfly.

The butterfly flew ahead, helping pull the leaf along.

For the first time since waking, Thumbelina laughed.

<!-- block:lifted-away -->
Then something buzzed overhead.

A great beetle swooped down, caught Thumbelina around the waist and carried her high into a tree.

The river disappeared beneath the leaves.

The butterfly and the floating leaf vanished into the distance.

The beetle set Thumbelina on a branch.

“You’re very unusual,” he said.

Soon other beetles gathered around.

They looked at Thumbelina’s two legs.

Her smooth face.

Her lack of feelers.

Her tiny hands.

“She doesn’t look like us at all,” one beetle said.

“How strange,” said another.

Thumbelina’s cheeks grew hot.

The first beetle had thought she was wonderful when he found her.

Now, because everyone else was laughing, he began to look embarrassed.

At last he carried her down from the tree and left her beside a daisy.

Thumbelina watched him fly away.

Then she sat beneath the flower and cried.

Not because she wished she looked like a beetle.

Because she had started to wonder whether there was anywhere in the enormous world where she would fit.
