# South with the Swallow

The swallow flew high above the fields.

Thumbelina held tightly to his feathers.

They crossed forests glowing red and gold.

They crossed mountains where snow shone on the peaks.

They flew beyond the cold winds and into warm air scented with flowers.

Below them grew oranges, grapes and great green trees.

For the first time, Thumbelina saw a world that seemed made to her size.

Flowers opened like rooms.

Petals curved like chairs.

Tiny paths ran between stems.

The swallow landed beside a white flower near an old marble column.

“This,” he said, “might be a good place to begin.”

<!-- block:the-flower-people -->
Thumbelina stepped onto a leaf.

From the surrounding flowers came tiny people no taller than she was.

Some had bright wings.

Some wore crowns made from petals.

Nobody stared at Thumbelina’s height.

Nobody laughed because she looked different.

One small flower prince approached her.

“Welcome,” he said. “Would you like to stay?”

Thumbelina looked around.

“Can I decide later?”

“Of course.”

That answer made her smile.

So she stayed for a while.

She made friends.

She learned the paths.

She visited the swallow whenever he returned.

And only when she was certain that this was where she wanted to be did she choose to make it her home.

<!-- block:her-own-place -->
The flower people gave Thumbelina a pair of tiny wings.

She flew from blossom to blossom.

Sometimes she thought about the walnut-shell bed beside the old window.

Sometimes she remembered the river, the beetles, the winter burrow and the tunnels below the earth.

They had all been parts of her journey.

But none of them had been allowed to decide its ending.

Thumbelina had crossed a world that was enormous compared with her.

She had been frightened.

She had been lonely.

She had also been brave.

And in the end she found something even better than a place where she fitted.

She found a place she had chosen for herself.
