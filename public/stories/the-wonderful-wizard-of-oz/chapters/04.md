# The Cowardly Lion

Deeper in the forest came a tremendous roar.

A huge Lion leapt onto the road.

He knocked the Scarecrow over and rushed towards the Tin Woodman.

Then he turned towards Toto.

Dorothy stepped in front of her little dog.

“Don't you dare!”

The Lion stopped.

Dorothy told him that frightening someone so much smaller than himself was cowardly.

To everyone's surprise, the Lion began to cry.

“I know,” he admitted. “I am a coward.”

He explained that everyone expected lions to be brave.

He could roar loudly enough, but inside he was often frightened.

<!-- block:a-fourth-wish -->
Dorothy told him they were going to see the Wizard of Oz.

The Lion's ears lifted.

“Could he give me courage?”

“Perhaps.”

So the Cowardly Lion joined them.

Soon the road became difficult and dangerous.

The Lion carried his friends across a wide gap.

Later, when strange creatures threatened them, he stood between the danger and Dorothy even though his legs were shaking.

Dorothy began to wonder about him too.

<!-- block:the-poppy-field -->
Eventually the forest opened into a huge field of red poppies.

The flowers smelled wonderful.

Too wonderful.

Dorothy became sleepy.

So did Toto.

Then the Lion.

The Scarecrow and Tin Woodman did not need to breathe, so the flowers did not affect them.

They carried Dorothy and Toto out of the field.

The Lion was too heavy.

Fortunately, thousands of field mice came to help after the Tin Woodman had protected their queen.

Together they pulled the sleeping Lion to safety.

When he woke, the Lion thanked them all.

“I was completely helpless.”

“You have helped us plenty of times,” Dorothy said.

The four friends continued towards the Emerald City.
