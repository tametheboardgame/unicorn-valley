# The Cyclone

Dorothy lived in Kansas with Uncle Henry, Aunt Em and her little black dog, Toto.

Their house stood alone on a wide, flat prairie. There were no trees nearby and very little colour. The sun had baked the grass grey, and the wind had faded the paint from the house.

Dorothy still found plenty to smile about because Toto made her laugh.

One day Uncle Henry looked at the sky and hurried towards the animals.

Aunt Em looked too.

“Cyclone!” she called.

There was a storm shelter beneath the house, and Aunt Em climbed down quickly.

Dorothy ran to follow her.

Then Toto jumped from her arms.

She caught him, but before she could reach the shelter the whole house began to shake.

<!-- block:the-house-rises -->
A powerful wind lifted the house straight into the air.

Dorothy fell onto the floor.

Then something astonishing happened.

The house stopped shaking quite so violently.

It was still moving, but it seemed almost to be floating.

Dorothy looked through a window and saw nothing but darkness and rushing wind.

She held Toto close.

There was nowhere else to go, so eventually she lay on her bed and fell asleep.

Hours later came a tremendous bump.

The house had landed.

<!-- block:munchkin-country -->
Dorothy opened the door.

Kansas had vanished.

Outside was a bright country filled with green fields, flowers, trees and birds.

A group of small people approached, along with a kindly old woman dressed in white.

They thanked Dorothy.

She had no idea why.

During the landing, her house had fallen on the Wicked Witch of the East, who had ruled the Munchkins cruelly.

Dorothy was horrified.

“I didn't mean to hurt anybody. I only want to go home.”

The old woman introduced herself as the Good Witch of the North.

She gave Dorothy the dead witch's silver shoes and told her there might be one person powerful enough to help.

“The great Wizard of Oz lives in the Emerald City.”

“How do I get there?”

“Follow the road of yellow brick.”

Dorothy put on the silver shoes, picked up a basket and called Toto.

Then the two of them began their journey.
