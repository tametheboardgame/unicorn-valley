# The Tin Woodman

The road led into a thick forest.

Dorothy, Toto and the Scarecrow heard a strange groaning sound.

They followed it and discovered a man made entirely of tin.

He stood perfectly still with an axe raised in one hand.

“Oil can,” he whispered.

Dorothy found an oil can inside a nearby cottage.

She oiled his neck.

Then his arms.

Then his legs.

At last he moved with a great creak.

“Thank you,” he said. “I've been standing there for a very long time.”

<!-- block:a-man-made-of-tin -->
The Tin Woodman explained that he had once been an ordinary man.

Piece by piece, accidents and magic had led to his body being replaced with tin.

He had survived, but he believed something important had been lost.

“I have no heart,” he said sadly.

Dorothy told him about the Wizard.

“If he can send me home and give the Scarecrow brains, perhaps he can give you a heart.”

The Tin Woodman joined them.

<!-- block:a-gentle-traveller -->
As they travelled, Dorothy noticed that the Tin Woodman was extraordinarily careful with every living creature.

He moved insects out of the road so nobody would step on them.

He felt sorry for damaged flowers.

He worried whenever anyone was frightened.

Once, when he accidentally trod on a beetle, he was upset for the rest of the afternoon.

The Scarecrow tried to comfort him.

“You seem to care a great deal for someone without a heart.”

“That is why I want one so badly,” said the Tin Woodman.

Dorothy exchanged a glance with the Scarecrow.

Neither of them said what they were thinking.
