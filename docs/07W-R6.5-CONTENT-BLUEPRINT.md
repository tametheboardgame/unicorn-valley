# R6.5 - Concrete Content Blueprint

## Purpose

`07V-R6.5-VALLEY-COMPLETENESS-BREADTH.md` defines the release contract and work packages. This document turns that contract into a concrete creative target so later implementation packages do not have to invent the valley's missing life one PR at a time.

This is a **working production blueprint**, not an excuse to ignore what already exists. R6.5-WP1 must reconcile every proposed name, quest, landmark and mechanic against the current content registry, save schema and map geometry before implementation. Where an existing identity already fills a role, reuse it rather than creating a duplicate.

The objective is density with personality: more unicorns, more little reactions, more reasons to stop, more stories, more things to spend rewards on and more activities worth revisiting.

---

# What "more detail" means

R6.5 should avoid solving emptiness by simply adding more scenery.

A richer area should contain a mixture of:

- **visual detail** - props, animation, foreground/background life and clearer landmarks;
- **social detail** - named residents, visitors, moving characters and changing dialogue;
- **interactive detail** - things that react when touched/approached;
- **story detail** - quests, microstories, follow-ups and persistent consequences;
- **collection detail** - secrets, discoveries and region-specific finds;
- **activity detail** - something the player can deliberately do again;
- **revisit detail** - a reason the second or fifth visit is not identical to the first.

An interaction does not need to become a quest. A child tapping a fountain and making three rainbow fish leap out is worthwhile world content even if it awards nothing.

Conversely, a prop should not advertise interaction if it has none. The game should build a reliable curiosity language.

# Supporting resident roster

The six core characters remain the hero cast. R6.5 adds a lighter layer of recurring residents so the valley feels inhabited without requiring ten more bespoke protagonist arcs.

The following are **working-name/role candidates**. WP1 must first check existing race/NPC IDs and should promote an existing identity where one already matches the role. # Region blueprint

## Moonflower Glade

### Desired feeling

Home is peaceful, but not empty. It should feel like the one place the player knows intimately and therefore notices when small things change.

### Landmark upgrades

- **Hollow Tree Nook** - pay off the mysterious hollow tree as a small secret/micro-location, with a visible change after discovery;
- **Moonflower Bridge** - clearer scenic identity plus water/flower reactions;
- **Garden Corner** - visually reflects Willow/home progress without prematurely becoming the full gardening system;
- **Discovery Display** - outdoor stump/shelf/display that gains objects from quests and regions.

### Small interactions

Candidate set:

1. tap the stream to make ripples/fish/sparkles;
2. toss/skitter a pebble from a marked bridge spot;
3. touch Moonflowers at night and make them glow in sequence;
4. inspect the hollow-tree markings before it opens;
5. sit/pose at the bridge or cottage step;
6. follow Juniper's butterfly for a short visual reveal;
7. tap the outdoor display to inspect earned discoveries;
8. ring/touch a cottage wind-chime or bell and get a small animation;
9. react to visitor footprints/notes when a friend is visiting;
10. trigger a tiny firefly cluster at dusk/night.

Not all need rewards. Several should simply make the home respond.

### Story candidates

- **Pip: The Hollow Tree Whispers** - investigate marks/sparkles, ask one other resident, find a tiny hidden nook and leave it visibly opened afterwards;
- **Juniper: The Butterfly That Knows the Way** - follow a distinctive butterfly through two familiar Glade landmarks to a hidden flower/discovery;
- **Willow follow-up: Moonflowers After Dark** - use the existing planted Moonflowers plus a night interaction to reveal a new memory/display item rather than another fetch quest.

### Revisit states

- visitors appear after friendship thresholds;
- Moonflowers visibly change after Willow progression;
- hollow-tree nook stays discovered;
- outdoor display fills up;
- strange egg/companion state remains visible at home;
- ribbons/trophies can be inspected in the Cottage.

## Moonflower Cottage

### Desired feeling

Not a menu with walls around it. It should feel like the player's personal room where adventures leave physical traces.

### Interactions

- sit on bed/chair/cushion where present;
- inspect ribbon/trophy display;
- tap placed decorations for tiny reactions where practical;
- inspect the egg/companion area;
- switch a lamp/light object on/off without changing global time;
- view a memory/display shelf;
- friend visitor dialogue can comment on current decoration/theme;
- selected earned objects should have one-line child-readable descriptions.

### Scope guard

Do not turn every furniture item into a physics toy. A few authored reactions across major categories are enough to make the room feel tactile.

## Sunbeam Village

### Desired feeling

The busiest place in the current valley. On arrival there should normally be multiple unicorns visible and at least one thing happening that is not waiting specifically for the player.

### Square interactions

1. fountain splash/rainbow-fish reaction;
2. sundial sparkle/time-state reaction;
3. notice board with rotating optional ideas/events;
4. bench/seat interaction;
5. bunting/flags animate more strongly during events;
6. shop window preview/tap response;
7. resident cluster conversation or greeting reaction;
8. small lost-item/inspectable spot that changes with quests;
9. event decoration point that reflects picnic/Cup state;
10. Story House exterior map/sign interaction.

### Population pattern

Typical normal state should mix:

- one or two core characters where story-appropriate;
- two to four supporting residents across square/interiors;
- at least one resident walking/idling rather than all facing the player;
- occasional location swaps, e.g. Maple at Bakery in one state and Meadow picnic hill in another.

### Shop design

#### Twinkle & Thread

Purpose:

- primary cosmetic/wearable shop.

Stock families:

- bows/clips;
- neck ribbons/scarves;
- flower accessories;
- small race-themed wearables;
- region-themed items unlocked through discovery;
- later magical-effect items only if existing render architecture supports them cleanly.

Stock tiers:

- starter affordable stock;
- items unlocked after first race/first region discovery;
- themed items unlocked by quest/Cup/Beach progression;
- some reward-exclusive items remain non-purchasable so achievements still matter.

#### Sunbeam Bakery

Purpose:

- Marigold social hub;
- baking/decorating activity;
- decorative/gift/treat stock without introducing hunger meters.

Potential stock:

- picnic cakes/buns used as optional social gifts/events;
- table/cottage food decorations;
- themed picnic baskets/cloths;
- recipe/story cards as Wonderbook memories;
- occasional event item unlocked by quest rather than grind.

Buying food must never create maintenance gameplay. There is no hunger system.

#### Story House

Purpose:

- discovery/lore/map/clue hub, not a third generic shop.

Repeat-use ideas:

- Tansy/Lumi can reveal optional clue cards after discoveries;
- collected story scraps unlock short illustrated memories;
- region map postcards highlight landmarks already heard about without exposing every secret;
- the player can revisit completed story pages;
- selected decorative book/poster items can be exchanged/bought without locking core stories behind Shimmer.

Core progression information should never require a purchase.

### Village story candidates

- **Tansy: Lost Map Corners** - map fragments have ended up in three already-useful locations; each clue uses a different interaction rather than raw collection;
- **Marigold/Maple: The Wobbly Cake Plan** - choose/decorate a bake, carry the result into a social scene and see the chosen design reflected there;
- **Pebble: Little Fixes, Big Difference** - inspect two or three damaged/odd Village props, help Pebble choose fixes, then leave visible repaired variants;
- supporting-resident microstories around shop stock, fountain or notice-board events.

# Starlight Beach blueprint

## Desired feeling

The first genuinely new R6.5 region should immediately make the valley feel larger. It needs a very different silhouette and palette from the green inland regions, plus enough content on day one that the player does not walk around it once and leave.

## Major subareas

### Shell Cove

- calmer curved shoreline;
- Coral's usual location;
- shell collection/beachcombing activity start;
- small sheltered nook or cave-like rock pocket.

### Tide Pools

- several large readable pools with animated tiny creatures/sparkles;
- touch interactions;
- Ripple can visit later;
- region discovery set.

### Star Dunes

- warm sand/dune grass;
- Skipper's kites/flags;
- beach race entrance/hub;
- sandcastle interaction/activity scenery.

### Moonlit Point

- small viewpoint/secret pocket;
- stronger evening sparkle/reflection treatment;
- one progression/time-sensitive discovery;
- visible distant-world promise without requiring sea travel.

## Beach interactions

1. pick/inspect authored shells;
2. tide-pool creature peeks out;
3. make footprints/sand puff at marked soft-sand spots;
4. build/change a small sandcastle through a simple interaction;
5. kite/flag reacts to touch/wind ambience;
6. wave washes over a safe shoreline strip with visual response;
7. driftwood chime or shell mobile plays sound;
8. hidden sparkle appears in a rock pool after another interaction;
9. sit at Moonlit Point;
10. shoreline reflections change at sunset/night;
11. Coral comments on current collection;
12. race flags/banners appear after the beach course unlock.

## Beach story candidates

### Coral: Shells With Stories

Not a "collect ten shells" quest. The player finds three deliberately placed unusual shells, then uses Story House/Coral interactions to discover what visual pattern each represents. Completion adds the shells to a display/Wonderbook page and changes Coral's cove display.

### Skipper: Flags for the Shore Sprint

Help Skipper test three race-marker positions by visiting/activating beach landmarks. The chosen final banner style can be cosmetic and persists around the beach race start.

### Ripple follow-up: Brook Meets Sea

After Crystal Song, Ripple visits the tide pools and discovers that water makes a different set of sounds here. A short cross-region follow-up connects old and new content.

## Beach collection family

Working family: **Starlight Shells**.

Principles:

- small finite authored set, not random grind;
- distinctive silhouettes/colours;
- some found through ordinary exploration, some through tide-pool activity and one or two through stories;
- Wonderbook page shows silhouettes for missing examples;
- selected shells can appear in Cottage/Coral display.

# Repeatable activity blueprint

## 1. Firefly Lantern

Existing activity retained and better grounded physically in Whispering Woods through Fern/Firefly Grove/world-state hooks.

## 2. Marigold's Decorating Table

Location:

- Sunbeam Bakery.

Core loop:

- choose a base cake/bun/cookie-like shape;
- choose 2-3 toppings/colours/decorations;
- instantly see the result;
- optional "make another";
- first/completion results can create a Wonderbook memory or decorative/gift variant.

Design rules:

- no fail state;

- touch-first large choices;
- enough combinations to invite replay;

Location:

Core loop:

- short visual search across a bounded beach pocket;
- choose/spot several visible shells or tide-pool discoveries;
- no random ultra-rare grind;
- collection result feeds Starlight Shell Wonderbook page;

- gentle pace;
- clear touch targets;
- no punishment for missed items;
- rewards useful but not mandatory.

# Economy and reward blueprint

## What Shimmer is for

Shimmer should support choice and self-expression, not gate the story.

Primary sinks:

- Twinkle & Thread cosmetics;
- selected Cottage decorations;
- Bakery decorative/gift items;
- selected Story House decorative pages/posters/maps if useful;
- region-themed cosmetic stock unlocked by discovery.

Primary earn sources:

- quest/story completion;
- race completion/placement bonuses;
- first discovery/secret milestones;
- repeatable activities with modest capped-per-run rewards if needed;
- Cup milestones.

## Balance intention

- the player should be able to buy something appealing relatively early;
- a single desired ordinary item should not require repeated farming of one activity;
- expensive showcase items can create a medium-term goal but should not dominate play;
- story-critical objects are never paywalled;
- unique quest/ribbon rewards should remain special rather than all becoming shop stock.

WP3 should turn these principles into actual numbers using current reward rates and inventory data.

# Ambient-life rules

More unicorns only improve the world if they behave believably enough at gameplay scale.

Supporting residents should use a lightweight shared behaviour vocabulary:

- idle/look around;
- short wander within safe authored bounds;
- look/react when the player approaches;
- move to a second authored location under a world-state condition;
- talk with 2-3 meaningful dialogue states;
- optionally perform a tiny repeated action such as reading, arranging bread, watching a pond or adjusting race flags.

Avoid:

- NPCs wandering into gates or blocking paths;
- ten characters pacing randomly at once;
- residents whose only purpose is one generic sentence forever;
- visually identical clones differentiated only by names;
- crowd density that obscures the player's unicorn on a phone.

The player should recognise recurring residents because she has seen them elsewhere, not because the game explains an NPC system.

---

# Interaction-language rules

R6.5 adds many more interactive things, so interaction readability must remain consistent.

- touch targets must be physically generous even when the visual object is small;
- approaching an important interactive object should produce an obvious child-readable action cue where required;
- tiny optional environmental toys may use sparkle/motion/hover language rather than permanent text labels;
- tapping a non-active world object must not silently swallow click-to-move;
- interactions should normally give immediate visual/audio feedback within a fraction of a second;
- one interaction should not lock the player into a long modal unless it genuinely starts dialogue/activity;
- environmental interactions should remain usable in portrait mobile layouts;
- repeated interactions may vary a reaction once or twice where cheap, but endless random dialogue is unnecessary.

---

# What R6.5 should deliberately NOT become

To protect scope and the child-first design, this release is not:

- a full gardening simulator;
- a deep crafting/recipe economy;
- a hunger/energy/daily-needs system;
- an MMO-style town full of dozens of filler NPCs;
- an open-world procedural quest generator;
- a grind-heavy currency expansion;
- a flight release;
- a full companion overhaul;
- a real-world daily/seasonal timer system;
- a demand for every prop to be clickable.

The target is a **dense, authored small game** with enough breadth that the next preference review becomes meaningful.
