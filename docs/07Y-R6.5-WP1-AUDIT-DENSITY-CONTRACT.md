# R6.5-WP1 - Valley Content Audit and Density Contract

## Status

**COMPLETE - implementation baseline locked for R6.5-WP2 onward.**

This document is the verified R6.5 starting-state audit required by `07V-R6.5-VALLEY-COMPLETENESS-BREADTH.md`.

It reconciles the release plan and concrete blueprint against the production code after the R6-WP6.18 human gate. Later R6.5 packages should use this document instead of estimating what the game already contains.

The key audit finding is that raw registry counts overstate breadth. Unicorn Valley already contains a respectable number of discoveries and several story systems, but much of that content is sequential, one-shot or concentrated into a small number of locations. The R6.5 problem is therefore not simply "add more markers". It is to increase simultaneous choice, social life, environmental play, repeat use and reasons to revisit.

---

# 1. Counting rules

The following counting rules are authoritative for R6.5 planning.

## Meaningful quest/story thread

Counts when it provides a player-facing authored story with a beginning/progression/payoff or a comparably substantial discovery-story chain.

Does **not** count:

- technical/demo quests;
- legacy definitions no longer representing a substantive player-facing thread;
- a single dialogue line;
- a single collectable;
- a shop transaction;
- an ordinary region entry.

## Production race course

Counts a distinct repeatable course/course experience intended as normal player content.

Nova's mandatory first-run tutorial is tracked separately because it uses the Rainbow Run family as a tutorial experience rather than adding another production course to the valley.

## Repeatable non-racing activity

Counts a deliberate activity the player can choose to play again with meaningful variation, performance, progression, reward or mastery.

Ordinary shop browsing, dialogue and one-shot secrets do not count.

## Meaningful world interaction point

Counts a world-space reason to stop, inspect, play, talk, collect, enter or deliberately activate something.

For the R6.5 density contract:

- ordinary region exits do not count;
- global UI does not count;
- multiple copies of the same collectable can be listed as authored loci but do not create equivalent breadth;
- sequential quest markers are listed individually in the audit, but cannot be used to pretend that all are simultaneously available;
- a facade/interior with only a one-line response may be addressable but is marked content-light rather than treated as full-depth content;
- a persistent visual consequence counts as revisit/state depth, not as an additional interaction unless the player can actually interact with it.

This distinction is important. A region can contain eight authored marker locations and still feel empty if six disappear permanently after one visit.

# 4. Density heat map and release contract

## Heat-map scale

- **GREEN** - already broad enough that R6.5 work is primarily enrichment/integration.
- **AMBER** - meaningful content exists, but breadth/repeatability/social/environmental dimensions are thin.
- **RED** - major dead zones, one-purpose structure or too few simultaneous choices.
- **BLACK** - not yet built.

## Baseline heat map

| Area | Baseline | Primary weakness | R6.5 owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Moonflower Glade | AMBER | sequential secrets/story outweigh tactile optional play | WP5, WP11, WP13 |
| Moonflower Cottage | AMBER | decorating is real, ordinary objects/display depth is thin | WP5, WP15 |
| Sunbeam Village | RED/AMBER | supporting population and repeat-use interiors are missing | WP4, WP11, WP14 |
| Rainbow Meadow | RED | large traversal area with too few stopping points | WP6, WP11 |
| Rainbow Run | RED place / AMBER activity | activity router, not a living place; only one production course family | WP6, WP12 |
| Crystal Brook | RED/AMBER | one-shot/duplicate collectable density, weak ordinary life | WP7, WP11 |
| Whispering Woods | AMBER exploration / RED social | good secrets, low ordinary population and post-secret reuse | WP8, WP11, WP14 |
| Starlight Beach | BLACK | region absent | WP9, WP10, WP12, WP14, WP15 |

## Hard per-region release floor

Every main outdoor region must finish R6.5 with all of the following:

- at least **8 meaningful world interaction points**, excluding ordinary exits and global UI;
- at least **3 optional environmental toys/observations** that are not simply quest markers;
- at least **2 secrets/discoveries/collection opportunities**;
- at least **1 quest/story beat** that materially uses the region;
- at least **1 reason to revisit** after its first story pass;
- at least **1 visible or dialogue reaction to persistent state**;
- at least **1 strong visual landmark**;
- no major region may contain more than roughly **2 consecutive screenfuls** with nothing meaningful to do or notice.

## Planning target above the hard floor

The release floor is not the design target. To avoid later packages building exactly eight token interactions, use the following planning targets:

- Moonflower Glade: **10+** meaningful points, with at least 4 non-story environmental/social interactions;
- Sunbeam Village: **12+** meaningful points across square, residents and interiors;
- Rainbow Meadow: **10+** meaningful points before counting ordinary region exits;
- Rainbow Run: at least **4 hub/presentation/social reasons** around its racing role plus the race content itself;
- Crystal Brook: **10+** meaningful points with greater verb variety than collect/inspect;
- Whispering Woods: **10+** meaningful points with at least 2 recurring social/repeat-use elements;
- Starlight Beach: **10+** meaningful points at first production completion rather than launching at the eight-point minimum.

The final WP16 audit must check both the numeric floor and the distribution/verb-quality rule. Passing the number while leaving long dead corridors is a failure.

# 8. Current progression and unlock map

## World traversal spine

The broad current world route is:

`New Game -> Unicorn Creator -> Moonflower Glade -> Sunbeam Village -> Rainbow Meadow -> Crystal Brook -> Whispering Woods`

Moonflower Glade to Village, Village to Meadow, Meadow to Brook and Brook to Woods are ordinary world-travel connections rather than late-game progression locks.

This means R6.5 content should not accidentally turn exploration breadth into a long mandatory linear campaign. New regions/activities may have gentle story introductions, but the life-sim world should remain easy to wander.

## Opening dependency

- the first Moonflower Sparkle discovery establishes the opening progression state;
- Pip's Strange Egg world chain is held back until that first discovery exists.

## Rainbow Run dependency

On entering Rainbow Run:

- if Nova's first-race story is not yet ready, the player is routed to Nova story content;
- when the quest is ready to race, the player enters Nova's tutorial first run;
- after Nova's story is complete, the same entry routes to Sunrise Sprint.

## Crystal Cascade dependency

Crystal Cascade is the current explicit race progression lock.

- before Nova's story unlocks Sunrise Sprint, the clue asks the player to race with Nova first and then finish Sunrise Sprint;
- after Sunrise Sprint is unlocked but unfinished, the clue asks the player to finish Sunrise Sprint;
- finishing Sunrise Sprint unlocks Crystal Cascade;
- once Crystal Cascade itself has been finished it remains available.

R6.5-WP12 must preserve this simple readable progression unless it deliberately replaces it with the Rainbow Cup structure and migration/tests.

## Pip egg dependency

- first discovery -> Pip egg story can begin;
- four Glade clue/discovery steps lead to the Strange Egg;
- after the egg is found, subsequent meaningful activity types can advance egg growth between sessions;
- the Cottage displays the current egg stage;
- hatch-ready transitions into the hatch scene;
- Luma persists afterwards.

This is an existing example of cross-system revisit design. R6.5 should reuse the principle, not copy the exact mechanic.

## Secret-chain dependencies

Existing examples include:

- Glade secret prerequisites leading to Moonlit Petal Trail;
- Brook music clues leading to Crystal Brook Water Song and a revealed trail/state;
- Woods clue chain leading to the Starwell and Lumi;
- Mossy Whisper Path revealing a persistent route;
- Pebble's three-region curiosity sequence leading to a repaired Village state.

These demonstrate that the save/world-flag architecture already supports persistent consequences. R6.5-WP11/WP13 should add visible consequences using the existing architecture rather than inventing a parallel state system.
