# R6.5 - Valley Completeness and Breadth

## Purpose

R6.5 is a committed content-completion release inserted between R6 and R7.

R6 proved that Unicorn Valley can look, control and deploy like a real child-facing game. The final R6 human playthrough also showed that the build is now stable enough to expand without spending another release on foundations. However, the current valley is still too sparse to support a meaningful daughter-led preference review. A child choosing between a small number of quests, two race courses, lightly populated regions and only partially useful shops is not yet choosing between mature play fantasies. She is mostly choosing between whichever systems currently have the most content.

R6.5 therefore does **not** attempt to predict which long-term fantasy should dominate the game. Its job is to make the existing game broad, populated and complete enough that R7 preference evidence becomes trustworthy.

The release should make the valley feel like a small but genuine open-ended game rather than a polished collection of vertical-slice systems.

R7-WP7.1 remains blocked until R6.5-WP17 is explicitly released by human playthrough.

---

# Release outcome

At the end of R6.5 a player should be able to open Unicorn Valley without being funnelled into one obvious piece of content and have several credible answers to "What shall I do?":

- explore a place she has not fully searched;
- talk to unicorns who are doing things of their own;
- follow or continue a quest;
- go shopping and have something worth spending rewards on;
- change her unicorn or decorate home;
- race on several genuinely different courses;
- work through a friendly cup/championship;
- play a non-racing activity;
- collect something for the Wonderbook;
- investigate a secret or environmental interaction;
- visit a familiar region and find changed dialogue, visitors or world state;
- travel to a genuinely new region.

The release is successful when these choices feel like parts of one living valley rather than disconnected demos.

# R7 readiness minimums

These are release-level minimums rather than suggestions. R6.5-WP1 may raise them after the content audit if the existing game is further from completeness than expected.

## Places and exploration

By the R6.5 gate:

- Moonflower Glade, Sunbeam Village, Rainbow Meadow, Rainbow Run, Crystal Brook and Whispering Woods all receive a deliberate content-density pass;
- Starlight Beach is added as a full new production region;
- Moonflower Cottage remains a meaningful home rather than only a decoration screen;
- at least **four new discrete sublocations/interiors/secret spaces** are created inside the existing valley, using existing visible promises where possible;
- every large outdoor region contains at least three recognisable named landmarks or subareas with an actual gameplay purpose;
- no major outdoor region should contain more than roughly two consecutive screenfuls of travel with nothing meaningful to inspect, collect, talk to, discover or do.

Candidate existing-world sublocations include, subject to WP1 audit and collision/layout validation:

- the mysterious hollow tree in Moonflower Glade;
- a real windmill/lookout or equivalent landmark in Rainbow Meadow;
- a crystal grotto/cave pocket in Crystal Brook;
- a deeper secret grove/mushroom-ring area in Whispering Woods.

These are not required to become enormous scenes. They are required to become places with a reason to exist.

## Region interaction-density contract

Each main outdoor region should finish R6.5 with, at minimum:

- **8 meaningful world interaction points**, excluding ordinary exits and global UI;
- at least 3 of those interactions must be optional environmental toys/observations rather than quest markers;
- at least 2 secrets, discoveries or collection opportunities;
- at least 1 quest or story beat that materially uses that region;
- at least 1 reason to revisit after the first successful visit;
- at least 1 visible or dialogue-level reaction to persistent world/player state;
- at least 1 strong landmark that helps a child orient herself without reading a map label.

A meaningful interaction can be small. Examples include ringing a bell, splashing a fountain, sitting at a viewpoint, making crystals sing, investigating unusual flowers, peeking into a hollow, following butterflies, opening a hidden cache, helping an NPC with a tiny problem, or triggering a short visual reaction. The important requirement is that exploration repeatedly answers curiosity.

Do not make every decorative prop interactive. Interaction density should make the world feel responsive, not turn it into visual noise.

## Unicorn population

R6.5 should add **8-10 supporting recurring unicorn residents** beyond the established core cast, using the modular production-unicorn system where practical rather than requiring bespoke hero art for every resident.

Supporting residents should:

- have a distinct name, appearance and one-line personality/gameplay identity;
- have at least 2-3 dialogue states rather than one permanent line;
- react to at least one progression, weather, time, quest or location condition where practical;
- be distributed across the valley instead of all living in Sunbeam Village;
- include some residents who appear in more than one location so the valley feels inhabited rather than staged;
- be usable by short quests, shops, races, activities or secrets without becoming mandatory exposition characters.

Population targets:

- Sunbeam Village should normally show several residents doing different things;
- Moonflower Glade should receive visitors as progression grows;
- Rainbow Meadow/Run should include racers, spectators, picnickers or wandering residents;
- Crystal Brook and Whispering Woods should no longer feel almost uninhabited;
- Starlight Beach should launch with its own local residents/visitors rather than only importing the core cast.

Ambient unicorns must not be immobile decorative clones. Even lightweight wander, idle, look/reaction and location-state behaviour is preferable to static crowd props.

## Quests and stories

By the final R6.5 gate:

- the game should contain at least **12 meaningful quest/story threads in total**;
- R6.5 should add at least **6 new substantive threads**, with the exact number raised if WP1 finds the current total below expectation;
- both short one-session stories and longer multi-region threads are desirable;
- core NPCs should receive follow-up content rather than only first-introduction quests;
- supporting residents should provide some smaller stories so every quest does not originate from the same six characters;
- at least two new threads should visibly change a place after completion;
- at least two should involve multiple characters or locations;
- at least one should be discoverable through exploration rather than a suggestion card.

New quests should not all be collection errands. Across the R6.5 quest set, use a mix of:

- exploration and clue following;
- finding or returning objects;
- choosing decorations or outcomes;
- using a shop or activity;
- racing/challenges;
- environmental interactions;
- simple making/baking;
- friendship follow-ups;
- secrets;
- persistent repairs or improvements;
- collection where collecting is thematically interesting.

A quest should normally combine at least two different verbs rather than "collect N items and return" being the whole experience.

## Racing

The current racing system needs enough content to be judged as an activity rather than a pair of examples.

R6.5 target:

- **5 total race courses** by release end;
- keep Sunrise Sprint and Crystal Cascade;
- add at least 3 genuinely distinct courses or course experiences;
- ensure new courses differ in scenery, obstacle rhythm and at least one mechanical/route characteristic rather than being palette swaps;
- add a friendly multi-race **Rainbow Cup/championship structure** using existing race results/ribbon systems where possible;
- include race unlocks/rewards that connect back to shopping, customisation, Wonderbook or home display;
- preserve participation-friendly design and assistance options;
- make losing a race produce useful progress rather than a dead end.

Working thematic candidates, not immutable names:

- a flower/meadow course;
- a winding Whispering Woods course;
- a Starlight Beach shoreline/tide course.

Course names and exact mechanics should be settled during R6.5-WP12 after reviewing what the current reusable race architecture supports cleanly.

## Shops and economy

Sunbeam Village must finally behave like a social/commercial hub rather than a set of attractive facades.

Required outcomes:

- Sunbeam Bakery, Twinkle & Thread and Story House each have a clear reason to enter repeatedly;
- at least two interiors provide real purchase/exchange flows;
- Twinkle & Thread remains the primary wearable/cosmetic shop;
- the Bakery connects Marigold, food/picnic content and a repeatable activity or useful decorative/gift stock;
- Story House becomes a useful story/discovery destination through Lumi, clues, Wonderbook-related content, maps, unlockable stories or another coherent non-duplicate role;
- shop stock grows through normal progression instead of appearing as one flat final list;
- owned items and sold-out/one-time items are handled clearly;
- touch purchasing is safe and cannot double-charge;
- prices and reward rates allow a child to buy desirable early items through normal play without grinding.

The economy should create choices, not chores. Currency/reward sources should include a healthy mix of quests, races, discoveries and repeatable activities. No required progression should depend on repetitive farming.

## Repeatable non-racing activities

By R6.5 completion the valley should offer at least **3 repeatable non-racing activities in total**, including the existing Firefly Lantern activity.

R6.5 therefore adds at least two more.

Preferred candidates:

- a simple Marigold baking/decorating activity tied to the Bakery;
- a Starlight Beach beachcombing/tide-pool/shell activity;
- a small Pebble making/display activity if scope permits and it can reuse existing item/decor systems.

Activities should:

- be understandable without a long tutorial;
- work with touch;
- give a small useful reward or collection result;
- support replay without daily timers or FOMO;
- remain optional.

## Wonderbook and long-term collection

The Wonderbook should become a reason to notice the breadth of the valley rather than simply an early tutorial object.

R6.5 should make it useful for tracking:

- characters met;
- places/landmarks discovered;
- secrets and unusual discoveries;
- ribbons/race achievements;
- creature/egg/companion discoveries where already supported;
- region-specific collectable families;
- selected memories/world changes.

The book should show enticing undiscovered spaces without turning play into an intimidating completion checklist. Progress feedback should remain positive and curiosity-led.

# Region-by-region expansion intent

## Moonflower Glade and Moonflower Cottage

R6.5 should make home feel increasingly personal and inhabited as the save progresses.

Targets:

- activate the hollow-tree promise as a real secret, micro-location or progression interaction;
- add more stream/bridge/garden/flower interactions;
- use the display stump/shelf or equivalent to show discoveries earned elsewhere;
- allow core/supporting NPCs to visit under clear conditions;
- add at least one new Glade-centred quest and one small discoverable secret chain;
- add cottage interactions beyond placement UI where useful, such as sitting/viewing displays, touching lamps/objects, checking the egg/companion space or inspecting earned trophies;
- improve visible ribbon/treasure/collection display so achievements return home with the player;
- add enough decorative/world-state variation that returning home after several quests feels different from the opening.

Do not implement the full future gardening system here unless a small reusable garden interaction falls naturally out of the work. Full gardening remains eligible for later daughter-led expansion.

## Sunbeam Village

This should be the busiest place in the launch-sized valley.

Targets:

- make all three authored interiors useful, not merely enterable;
- populate the square and shops with supporting residents;
- give the fountain/sundial, notice board, seating and shop displays small interactions;
- create shop stock/reward loops with visible progression;
- add conversations that change as quests and friendships advance;
- add at least two new Village-centred quest/story threads;
- allow some NPCs to move between square/interiors or appear in different contexts;
- make entering a shop feel visually and mechanically rewarding;
- avoid every NPC standing in an isolated fixed position waiting for the player.

## Rainbow Meadow and Rainbow Run

Targets:

- turn picnic hill, pond, flower circle and windmill/lookout promise into meaningful landmarks;
- increase optional flower/butterfly/rainbow-fragment interactions;
- add picnickers, racers, spectators and wandering residents;
- create at least one Meadow secret that changes by time/weather/progression;
- deepen Nova/racer follow-up dialogue and rival/friend interactions;
- strengthen the ribbon board as a real record of the player's racing progress;
- use this area as the social home of the Rainbow Cup;
- add at least one new Meadow/Run story unrelated to simply starting a race.

## Crystal Brook

Targets:

- add a crystal grotto/cave or equivalent discrete exploration pocket;
- make stepping stones, crystals, reflections, shells/water objects and waterfall areas meaningfully interactive;
- add supporting residents or visitors with reasons to be there;
- add at least one new quest/story thread and one secret/discovery chain;
- provide post-Crystal-Cascade reasons to return;
- let some crystals/environmental objects respond with light/sound rather than existing only as scenery;
- preserve clear path treatment established during R6-WP6.18 remediation.

## Whispering Woods

Targets:

- build out the glowing-mushroom, firefly, hidden-path and secret-grove fantasy;
- add a deeper grove/mushroom ring/ancient-tree-style landmark or micro-location;
- populate the woods with a small number of residents/visitors and creature activity while keeping the mystery intact;
- add at least one treasure/clue quest and another shorter interaction/story;
- expand Firefly Lantern's connection to the physical world;
- use night/time/weather conditions for optional discoveries;
- make the woods mysterious and inviting, never threatening or visually unreadable.

## Starlight Beach

Starlight Beach is promoted from future backlog into committed R6.5 scope because the valley needs another strongly different place before preference-led expansion.

Production targets:

- a full explorable beach region with a distinct palette, ambience and navigation language;
- shell cove/shoreline, tide-pool area, dunes/sand area and at least one sheltered/secret pocket;
- local or visiting supporting unicorns;
- a region-specific collectable family;
- at least two quest/story threads using the beach;
- a repeatable beachcombing/tide-pool/shell activity;
- at least two secrets/discoveries;
- a beach-themed race course;
- clear links back into the existing economy, Wonderbook and character network;
- visual promises for future sea/flight content without requiring those systems now.

The beach must launch with content. A beautiful empty map does not satisfy this package.

# Work packages

## R6.5-WP1 - Valley Content Audit and Density Contract

Dependencies: R6-WP6.18 human gate released

Goal:

- establish an exact baseline of what exists and convert the release-level targets in this document into a verified implementation checklist.

Deliverables:

- inventory of every current region, interior, NPC, quest, race, activity, shop, interactable, discovery and meaningful reward;
- region heat-map/list of dead or low-density areas;
- identification of dead facades, unused landmarks and one-line NPCs;
- exact current quest/race/activity counts;
- map of existing progression dependencies and unlock order;
- final supporting-NPC count and placement plan;
- candidate sublocation validation against current maps/collision;
- explicit R6.5 content matrix with planned owner WP for every gap;
- capture of minor R6 playthrough polish notes into the R6.5-WP16 backlog.

Acceptance:

- no later R6.5 package has to guess what content already exists;
- every main region has measurable before/after targets;
- the planned breadth is sufficient to make R7 preference evidence meaningful.

## R6.5-WP2 - Ambient Population and World Interaction Toolkit

Dependencies: R6.5-WP1

Goal:

- make it cheap and safe to add many believable residents and small world interactions without bespoke scene hacks.

Deliverables:

- reusable supporting-unicorn definition/presentation using modular production assets;
- lightweight idle/wander/look/reaction behaviours;
- conditional location/dialogue support where not already reusable;
- reusable small world-interaction patterns with touch-safe targeting;
- interaction feedback patterns for inspect/play/sit/ring/splash/listen/reveal-style actions;
- content validation for resident and interaction IDs;
- automated checks preventing prototype markers or tiny mobile targets from returning.

Acceptance:

- a new supporting resident can be defined largely through data/configuration;
- a new small environmental interaction does not require a bespoke interaction framework;
- residents remain performant and do not interfere with navigation.

## R6.5-WP3 - Economy and Reward Loop Completion

Dependencies: R6.5-WP1

Goal:

- ensure rewards from the existing game have useful purposes and shopping can expand without grind.

Deliverables:

- clear earn/spend balance bands;

- progression-sensitive shop stock rules;
- safe ownership/one-time-purchase handling;
- reward-preview language where useful;
- economy regression tests including reload and duplicate-tap safety.

Acceptance:

- normal varied play gives enough resources for desirable early purchases;
- no required progression depends on repetitive currency farming;
- rewards feel connected to the rest of the game rather than accumulating pointlessly.

- interactive fountain/sundial, notice board, seating and shop displays where appropriate;

Acceptance:

- the village visually contains multiple unicorns doing different things;
