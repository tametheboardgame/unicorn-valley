# R6.5-WP19H2 – Moonflower Cottage & Home Customisation

**Status:** In progress

**Parent release:** R6.5 – Valley Completeness and Breadth

**Previous package:** R6.5-WP19H1 – Moonflower Glade Final Polish

## Purpose

Rebuild Moonflower Cottage into a proportionate, detailed, readable and genuinely personal home space. The package must improve the room itself first, then layer sleeping and customisation onto a stable layout rather than decorating prototype geometry.

The implementation must follow the H1 engineering rule: superseded source behaviour is removed or reconfigured at its canonical owner. Do not solve cottage problems through create-then-hide presentation, delayed suppression, coordinate scans, cleanup timers or duplicate parallel systems.

## Current implementation findings

The present cottage is a 1800×1200 procedural room whose major furniture and colliders are hard-coded primitive shapes. Several visible issues are source-authored rather than incidental:

- the fireplace, bed, tea table, sofa and treasure shelf use simple fixed geometry and hard-coded collision rectangles;
- the bed is one solid collision block, so it cannot support a natural walk-into-bed interaction;
- the door explicitly renders a `Moonflower Glade` label above it;
- decorative moon glyphs are explicitly placed around the floor;
- every decoration slot is permanently registered as a normal interaction and empty slots are permanently rendered as visible purple `✦` markers;
- the HUD permanently tells the player to choose a decoration spot;
- the player and cottage objects use largely fixed depth values rather than robust spatial layering;
- the strange egg and cottage visitors use separately hard-coded coordinates, which makes a room redesign brittle;
- the existing decoration service/catalogue already provides useful persistence, ownership, compatibility and placement logic and should be retained rather than replaced;
- the existing atmospheric time system already has morning/afternoon/sunset/night states and should be extended for sleeping rather than duplicated;
- room-surface styling such as wallpaper, wall colour and flooring is not yet represented in the save schema and requires a proper versioned persistence extension.

## Product decisions

- The cottage should feel proportionate to the player unicorn rather than long, low and over-scaled.
- Permanent furniture should be redrawn in substantially greater detail with a more deliberate layout.
- The fireplace must remain fully readable beneath the game UI and use correct collision/layering.
- Normal play must show no decoration placement markers and decoration slots must not compete with ordinary interactions.
- A dedicated **Decorate** mode will reveal subtle blue magical placement affordances similar to the rest of the game.
- Wall colour, floor style, wallpaper and selected furniture finishes should be customisable.
- Customisation uses curated named styles rather than unrestricted RGB values so colours/patterns can be meaningful quest/shop rewards and retain art direction.
- Starter choices are available immediately; additional colours, wallpapers and variants can be unlocked by quests/rewards and later shops.
- Story objects such as the strange egg use dedicated semantic story anchors, not ordinary player-decoration slots.
- Reserve 4–5 semantic future story/display anchors and a dedicated future portal bay.
- The portal location is reserved architecturally in H2; fast-travel gameplay itself is deferred to a later package.
- The bed should support a Pokémon-like walk-in interaction: foreground and background pieces, unicorn head visible above the bedding, sleep trigger in the bed centre, fade to black, `A lovely night's sleep`, reset to morning, then fade back.
- Sound hooks may be left for the sleep sequence, but audio content is not required by this package.
- The existing HomeDecorationService/CottageDecorationCatalogue foundations should be evolved, not replaced with a second home system.

## Architectural constraints

1. **One visible owner.** Do not create old room/furniture/markers and hide them later.
2. **Semantic geometry.** Story content, visitors, sleep, decorations and future portal content bind to named anchors, not approximate coordinate searches.
3. **Normal-play cleanliness.** Decoration affordances exist only in Decorate mode.
4. **Separate story and decoration sockets.** Quest-critical content cannot be replaced by player decoration.
5. **Shared persistence.** Extend the versioned save schema rather than creating a cottage-only storage mechanism.
6. **Shared time system.** Sleeping resets the existing atmospheric time service to morning rather than maintaining another clock.
7. **Physical depth.** Furniture/player occlusion follows room geometry, not a growing list of arbitrary depth patches.
8. **Future-ready, not prematurely implemented.** Reserve the portal and future story anchors now; implement fast travel and later story systems in their own packages.
9. **Curated customisation.** Named styles and variants support unlock/reward design and preserve coherent art direction.
10. **Checkpoint delivery.** H2.0–H2.11 are independently reviewable. Stop at each human gate before the next numbered chunk unless David explicitly asks otherwise.

## Completion definition

H2 is complete only when:

- the rebuilt room proportions and furniture are human-approved;
- collision/layering are coherent;
- sleep reliably advances to morning;
- ordinary play has no decoration-slot clutter;
- Decorate mode cleanly owns all editing affordances;
- wall/floor/wallpaper and supported furniture styles persist safely;
- existing decoration inventory/placement semantics still work;
- egg and visitor systems bind to semantic anchors and remain functional;
- future story/display/portal space is reserved without implementing speculative gameplay;
- obsolete cottage prototype paths are removed rather than suppressed;
- full automated qualification passes;
- a deployed preview passes David’s final cottage playtest;
- David explicitly approves merge.
