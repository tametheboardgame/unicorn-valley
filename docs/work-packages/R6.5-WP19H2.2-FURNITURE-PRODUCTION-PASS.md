# R6.5-WP19H2.2 – Furniture Production Pass

## Purpose

Replace Moonflower Cottage's temporary H2.1 furniture blocks with permanent, deliberately styled room furnishings before collision and spatial layering are rebuilt in H2.3.

The user approved H2.1 on 16 September 2026 and then asked to start H2.3. The roadmap makes H2.3 depend on the final furniture silhouettes from H2.2, so H2.2 was completed first rather than building collision against temporary geometry and immediately discarding it.

David approved the finished H2.2 furniture pass on 16 September 2026 after the final visual cleanup of legacy overlays, wall/window layering and table construction.

## Scope

- substantially redesign the fireplace;
- substantially redesign the bed;
- substantially redesign the tea table and seating;
- substantially redesign the sofa/seating area;
- improve windows and curtains;
- improve the treasure shelf/display area;
- give the Wonderbook a deliberate reading nook rather than leaving it on an otherwise empty patch of floor;
- preserve the H2.1 open architectural exit instead of restoring a brown door panel;
- remove obsolete primitive furniture rendering from `CottageInteriorScene.ts` as the production renderer becomes canonical;
- preserve H2.1 furniture positions, interaction anchors and collision geometry for this visual-only checkpoint.

## Implementation ownership

- `CottageInteriorMap.ts` continues to own furniture positions and the H2.1 collision geometry until H2.3.
- `CottageFurnitureRenderer.ts` owns permanent-furniture presentation for H2.2 onward.
- `CottageInteriorScene.ts` owns scene composition and calls the dedicated furniture renderer rather than carrying individual primitive furniture methods.
- `WonderbookWorldProp.ts` remains the canonical Wonderbook prop renderer; H2.2 only adds its surrounding nook.

## Acceptance result

Approved by David on 16 September 2026.

The permanent furnishings are now sufficiently detailed and stylistically coherent to serve as the canonical visual silhouettes for H2.3 physical collision and front/back layering.
