# R6.5-WP17 - Full Human Playthrough and R7 Readiness Gate

## Objective

Verify through a real human playthrough that the completed R6.5 valley is broad, readable and coherent enough for later daughter-led preference evidence to be meaningful.

This is a hard human gate. It is not an autonomous implementation package.

## Canonical source

`docs/07V-R6.5-VALLEY-COMPLETENESS-BREADTH.md`, especially the R6.5-WP17 section and release-wide breadth minimums.

Playtest remediation authority after the 2026-09-05 human session: `docs/07Z-R6.5-WP17-PLAYTEST-REMEDIATION.md`.

## Dependency / entry condition

R6.5-WP16 is merged on `main` as `a424ec9f50be2d83b2a7c246e5735d9e24274480` after its final technical gate.

The first WP17 human playthrough has now occurred. It did **not** release R7.

## 2026-09-05 human playtest outcome

The substantially unguided daughter playthrough provided enough evidence to judge the current build. The underlying game showed strong engagement signals, but the gate failed because major usability/stability defects remain.

### Strong positive evidence

- player actively sought NPCs and missions to unlock more content;
- earning/spending currency and discovering things were enjoyable;
- Firefly activity was loved;
- Rainbow Meadow was visually liked;
- Whispering Woods was liked;
- Tree Nook and its mushrooms were strongly liked;
- tap-to-move was independently selected as the easiest movement method;
- player said she would play again.

### Gate-blocking evidence

- landscape-tablet controls and shell presentation are the dominant usability problem;
- racing is effectively blocked on the Samsung Galaxy Tab S8 because controls obscure the track and Chrome long-press behaviour interrupts RUN;
- tablet help is desktop/keyboard-oriented and confusing;
- UI text/boxes can overflow, overlap and create excessive visual noise;
- full freezes occurred in Tree Nook and Twinkle and Thread;
- opening the Bag at Sunlit Beach creates a repeatable world-disappearance/freeze failure;
- Echo cannot be spoken to;
- Moonflower Cottage collision/decoration behaviour is defective;
- Bag content beyond six visible items cannot be accessed;
- Food is collectible but unusable;
- purchase/reward feedback is unclear;
- Maple could not be found during active quest-seeking;
- NPC interaction rules are inconsistent;
- recurring characters can appear in mutually exclusive places simultaneously;
- several character/environment visual-quality problems remain.

Therefore the R7-readiness acceptance criteria are not met.

## Remediation path

Per the gate contract, bounded R6.5 remediation is now inserted as WP18A-H:

1. R6.5-WP18A - Landscape Tablet UX Concept and Approval.
2. R6.5-WP18B - Freeze Diagnostics, Root Cause and Stability.
3. R6.5-WP18C - Landscape Tablet Controls and Interaction Shell.
4. R6.5-WP18D - Playthrough Functional Bug and Regression Remediation.
5. R6.5-WP18E - Bag, Map, Creator and Modal Tablet UX.
6. R6.5-WP18F - World Consistency, Visual Quality and Experience Improvements.
7. R6.5-WP18G - Tablet Device Hardening and Cross-Input Regression.
8. R6.5-WP18H - Full Human Tablet Replay and Return to WP17.

WP17 remains open throughout remediation.

WP18H does **not** release R7. After the remediated human replay, the project returns here for the user's explicit readiness decision.

## Product-direction consequence

The playtest changes the interface/control design authority:

- landscape tablet is now primary;
- Samsung Galaxy Tab S8, Chrome, landscape is the reference human-test environment;
- tap-to-move is a first-class control scheme;
- desktop keyboard/mouse remains supported but secondary;
- the existing Phaser/TypeScript/Vite codebase remains authoritative;
- no native Android rewrite is authorised;
- later thin Android packaging of the existing build may be evaluated only after the remediated browser/tablet game is stable and accepted.

## Human playthrough questions for the next gate attempt

During the post-WP18 replay, assess:

1. Can the player find several different worthwhile things to do without adult direction?
2. Are landscape-tablet controls comfortable and obvious?
3. Can racing genuinely be played on the reference tablet?
4. Is child-facing help appropriate to the device actually being used?
5. Do all major regions feel populated and interactive rather than empty?
6. Are shops genuinely interesting and useful?
7. Is there enough quest/story content that questing feels like a real choice?
8. Are non-racing repeatable activities discoverable and worth replaying?
9. Does Starlight Beach feel complete and remain stable when Bag/Map/modals are used?
10. Do supporting residents make the valley feel inhabited and coherent rather than duplicated?
11. Does returning to older regions reveal changed states, dialogue or new reasons to visit?
12. Do remaining minor defects feel like tidy-up items rather than barriers to free play?

## Release acceptance

The human gate may be released only when all of the following are true:

- no progression blocker remains;
- no known hard freeze remains;
- no major landscape-tablet control/readability blocker remains;
- racing is genuinely playable by touch on the reference tablet;
- core interactions are understandable without desktop-control coaching;
- the player can spend multiple sessions choosing among genuinely different activities and places;
- the user explicitly confirms that the build is broad and usable enough for preference-led observation.

## Gate handling

- If the post-WP18 replay still finds blockers or meaningful R6.5 defects, keep WP17 unreleased and create bounded remediation.
- Minor non-blocking tidy-up items may be captured without forcing a redesign, but the user decides whether their combined impact blocks readiness.
- Do not interpret technical CI success as human acceptance.
- Do not infer acceptance from silence or from a single successful automated session.
- Only an explicit user readiness decision releases this gate.

## Technical invariants while remediating

- No intentional production deployment is authorised merely by this gate/remediation planning. Production deployment still requires explicit user approval.
