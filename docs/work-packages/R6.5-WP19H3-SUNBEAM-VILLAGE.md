David selected Sunbeam Village after H2 and supplied the first detailed feedback block on 21 September 2026. He then explicitly asked for analysis and a first-pass plan, approved that plan, and asked for it to be committed to the roadmap.

## Purpose

Use Sunbeam Village as the third human review lens in the open-ended R6.5-WP19H1+ final-polish programme and bring the village to the same finished standard as the accepted Moonflower Glade and Moonflower Cottage work.

Sunbeam should read as the valley's detailed, thriving social/commercial hub while remaining visually legible. More life and detail must come from deliberate spatial hierarchy, not from stacking additional independent overlays onto the existing scene.

The area boundary is not a technical boundary. If village feedback exposes a shared/global weakness, identify the canonical shared owner and implement the reusable correction rather than create a Sunbeam-specific workaround.

## Feedback block 1 - analysis findings

The clutter is structural rather than a single cosmetic defect. Sunbeam is currently composed by multiple independently positioned systems:

- the base scene draws the large rectangular village-square surface, broad roads, shop shells, bunting, floating labels and prototype NPC markers;
- production presentation later replaces/adds NPC art;
- environment production adds flower-box and ambient decoration;
- Village Life adds notice board, sundial, bench, map sign, shop-window display and fountain props;
- ambient-population systems add moving residents and small interactions.

Concrete confirmed artefacts include:

- a semi-transparent 1,220 × 690 rectangular square treatment that reads as an unexplained brown block;
- the old main west-east road polyline passing directly through the fountain centre;
- the surviving generic `shopkeeper-marker` star/label;
- overlapping activity around the northern shops from bunting, shop detail, village-life props, labels and resident routes;
- top-heavy density while the lower half of the map is comparatively empty.

The first pass is therefore a composition/layout rebuild that preserves underlying gameplay systems rather than a cosmetic patch over the current arrangement.

## Approved feedback-block-1 slices

### H3.1 - Legacy residue and composition ownership cleanup

- Remove the rectangular village-square treatment.
- Remove obsolete/duplicated path presentation, particularly geometry that runs through the fountain.
- Remove the surviving `shopkeeper-marker` presentation/topology where it no longer serves a valid gameplay purpose.
- Audit remaining prototype presentation against later production owners and remove genuinely superseded visual residue.
- Establish a clearer authoritative owner for Sunbeam-specific world composition so later H3 work does not become another presentation layer pasted over older layers.
- Preserve interactions, progression and resident authority while presentation ownership is consolidated.

### H3.2 - Village master layout rebuild

Recompose the full 3000 × 1900 village around recognisable districts:

- western Moonflower Glade approach;
- northern/high-street shop area;
- central fountain/plaza;
- south-western Willow/garden area;
- southern/eastern residential village;
- eastern Rainbow Meadow approach.

Rebalance the current top-heavy distribution, deliberately separate interaction hotspots and retain open movement/tap-navigation corridors. The village must feel connected rather than like isolated decorative islands.

### H3.2 structural ownership contract

The H3.2 remediation establishes one structural composition authority for Sunbeam Village:

- `SunbeamVillageLayout.ts` owns canonical coordinates, district geometry, the H3.3 plaza/path network and named visual-layer depths.
- `SunbeamVillageScene.ts` owns all static structural presentation: base/ground, districts, plaza/path network, shop exteriors, fountain, Willow garden, gateway presentation and simple ground accents.
- Each shop exterior is an atomic container rooted at its canonical building coordinate. Windows, awnings, door treatment and labels are children of that root and must not be independently positioned in world space.
- `WorldTraversalPolishManager` retains functional transition detection only for Sunbeam and must not draw Sunbeam paths or gateways.
- `ExplorationPathPolishManager` must not draw Sunbeam paths. H3.3 replaces the scene-owned scaffold in place rather than adding another path layer.
- `R65WorldExperiencePresentationManager` must not add Sunbeam shop exterior props. H3.8 will add final shop/village micro-detail through the H3 composition owner.
- `VisualTighteningManager` must not support Sunbeam Village. Its legacy fixed-coordinate shop-window pass was the source of the detached windows exposed after H3.2 moved the buildings.
- Dynamic systems remain separate by design: Core NPC presentation owns production NPC sprites, Ambient Population owns roaming residents, Village Life owns interactive props, and quest managers own conditional quest markers. Those systems consume canonical layout positions where they touch the rebuilt village.

This contract exists specifically to prevent the detached-window/path duplication failure exposed during H3.2 visual review.

### H3.3 - Proper plaza and path network

- Replace the hard-edged brown square with an organic storybook plaza/paving treatment.
- Give the fountain its own clear circular/organic focal area.
- Route the principal west-east path around the fountain rather than through it.
- Add deliberate branch paths to shops, Willow's garden and residential areas.
- Use path hierarchy and environment composition to communicate navigation without relying on floating labels.
- Preserve enough open space for player movement, tap navigation and roaming residents.

### H3.3 approval note

H3.3 was visually approved on 21 September 2026. The north Twinkle & Thread connection into the plaza is accepted for this pass, but David may ask to revisit that specific transition during a later H3 feedback block. It is not a blocker for H3.4.

### H3.4 - Shop exterior rebuild

Preserve the three existing destinations but give them genuinely distinct exterior identities instead of a repeated rectangle-plus-triangle template:

- Sunbeam Bakery: warm, welcoming, rounded/cottage-like bakery character;
- Twinkle & Thread: whimsical accessory/fashion identity;
- Story House: taller, bookish, old-storybook identity.

Vary silhouette, roof shape, frontage depth, windows, chimneys, trim, door treatment, landscaping and sign placement. Space the buildings as part of the master composition rather than a uniform row.

Existing interiors, shop/economy behaviour, inventories and entry/return behaviour remain preserved. Interior visual review is not part of this first-pass exterior package.

H3.4 visual refinement also brings the three shop-name signs forward from H3.5 because they are integral to the exterior compositions. Shop icon plaques, floating shop-name labels and `DOOR OPEN • COME IN` strips are removed in favour of façade-mounted signs. H3.5 remains responsible for the wider village wayfinding pass, including garden and gateway signage and other remaining environmental labels.

### H3.5 - Integrated signs and wayfinding

- Remove world-space environmental text boxes for shop names, garden labels and gateway directions.
- Remove cues such as `DOOR OPEN • COME IN` where the environment itself can communicate entry.
- Replace them with physical storybook signs: hanging shop signs, painted boards, carved posts, plaques and gateway signage.
- Rebuild the Moonflower Glade and Rainbow Meadow exits as visually legible physical gateways.
- Preserve the canonical global location HUD rather than treating all text as forbidden.

### H3.6 - Willow's garden district

- Move Willow and the garden further left/south-west so they read as an intentional village-edge district rather than an isolated object at the bottom of the map.
- Add a curved spur path from the main village circulation.
- Expand the garden with beds, planting, edging/fencing and restrained gardening detail.
- Preserve the existing planted/unplanted Moonflower story state.
- Integrate Willow's position, collision and interaction into the new composition.
- Use a physical garden sign/plaque rather than a floating label.

### H3.7 - Southern residential expansion

- Add a restrained set of additional homes/village buildings around the southern/eastern and/or south-western edges.
- Prefer buildings facing inward onto paths over a mechanically inverted bottom row.
- Use them to establish that Sunbeam is a lived-in village, not just three shops beside a fountain.
- These buildings do not require new interiors.
- Optional lightweight knock/inspect flavour may identify a resident or explain that the player needs an invitation for tea without implying inaccessible content is broken.
- Keep meaningful lawn, trees, paths and negative space between structures so additional detail does not recreate the existing clutter problem.

### H3.8 - Props, bunting, residents and village-life recomposition

- Reposition the notice board, map sign/chime, sundial, bench, fountain interaction and other Village Life props into deliberate micro-areas.
- Integrate the Twinkle & Thread window display into the building composition rather than letting a detached prop compete with the frontage.
- Anchor bunting between sensible structures and keep it clear of doors, signs, NPCs and important shop detail.
- Integrate flower boxes/environment detail into the relevant building/area composition instead of visually duplicating decoration from separate owners.
- Preserve moving supporting residents because they support the thriving-hub fantasy, but redesign relevant routes/pauses around the new paths and readable gathering areas.
- Maintain an uncluttered circulation ring around the fountain and shop entrances.
- Prefer environmental density around edges and authored pockets while leaving primary playable routes readable.
