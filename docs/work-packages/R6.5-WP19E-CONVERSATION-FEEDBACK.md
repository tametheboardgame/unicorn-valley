## Scope and execution contract conversation and feedback system

Autonomy: Amber visual/child-reading gate. Dependencies: WP19D. Findings: A04/A08/A15.

### Accepted presentation direction - clarified 2026-09-11

Ordinary NPC and quest conversations must remain in the active world scene. Do not transition into a separate conversation/cutscene scene merely to show dialogue.

- The player and active speaker remain visible in the world while talking.
- Dialogue owns the protected lower-screen speech area.
- Any contextual target/name/hint presentation that normally occupies that area is hidden for the complete lifetime of the conversation, so labels and speech never overlap.
- The contextual target/name/hint can return only after the close/advance input has been consumed and released, and only if the target remains eligible.
- Movement and background interaction are locked while speech is active. The engaged NPC stays stopped and facing the player until the conversation closes.
- Short speech, multi-line conversations, quest hand-ins and dialogue choices all use the same in-world visual family, expanding/reflowing as required rather than launching a separate cutscene screen.
- Existing conversation-only scene transitions should be retired as part of migration. Do not add a new ordinary-conversation return-position/checkpoint system to compensate for them.
- Dedicated scene transitions remain valid for genuine gameplay modes and activities where a separate scene is meaningful, for example races, minigames, creator flows or interiors/shops that already require scene ownership. Dialogue length alone is not a reason to leave the world scene.

This design deliberately removes the class of bugs where a conversation returns the player to an area's entrance instead of the exact place the NPC was engaged.

## Presentation types

- Short conversation: compact cream/lavender card above the control safe area, portrait or recognisable character icon, name, short line and clear cross/Done. Remains until dismissed; no 2.6-second reading limit.
- Multi-line/quest conversation: same visual family and same lower-screen owner, expands for text and choices; Continue advances, final acknowledgement closes. Choices use large separate buttons and never collide with body text.
- Outcome toast: nonessential collection/purchase/discovery acknowledgement, brief and queued/coalesced when several occur. Important progression detail remains in Book/quest state.
- Error/confirmation: explicit acknowledgement or choice; never disguised as a disappearing outcome toast.

## Behaviour

- Conversation lifetime controls the NPC pause and interaction lock. A short card does not require a full-screen dimmer merely because it contains speech.
- Allow manual close/back; suppress accidental click-through and immediate re-trigger on the same close input. Multi-step choice/effect execution stays in the existing dialogue/domain model.
- Use static readable text by default; any reveal animation must honour reduced motion and allow immediate reveal. Do not auto-advance speech.
- Keep the active speaker and relevant world visible. Reflow portrait and long text instead of shrinking to fit.
- Preserve quest-gated lines, friendship variants, choice effects and re-talk after completion. No new real-time/AI-generated dialogue service.
- The persistent top HUD remains available unless a genuine modal/gameplay mode has a separately approved reason to suppress it. Conversation placement must not collide with that HUD.

## Validation

Test single/long/multi-line speech, longest names, all choice counts in current content, rapid close/reopen, scene shutdown, NPC movement before/after, touch/keyboard, reduced motion, event exactly once and save/continue. Compare first Pip dialogue with a roaming resident and a quest hand-in.

Explicitly prove:

- no conversation-only scene transition occurs for migrated NPC/quest dialogue;
- player world position does not change because opening/closing dialogue does not leave the scene;
- contextual target/name/hint is absent while the conversation panel owns the lower speech area;
- the hint does not reappear or re-trigger until close input release;
- active NPC remains stopped/facing the player throughout;
- all current choice/effect/quest semantics still execute exactly once.

Done when brief and detailed speech feels like one conversation system, can be read at the child's pace, never overlaps target/hint UI, does not relocate the player, and cannot steal/double-trigger gameplay input.
