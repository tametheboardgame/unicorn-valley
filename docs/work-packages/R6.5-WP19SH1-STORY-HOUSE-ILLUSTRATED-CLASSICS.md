# R6.5-WP19SH1 - Story House Illustrated Classics Programme

## Purpose

Create a separate, repeatable content stream for reillustrating selected Story House classics with
new ChatGPT-generated artwork and materialising those images through the permanent R4 generated
asset pipeline.

This programme is intentionally independent from the main area/interior polish stream. Once R4 is
merged and accepted, SH1 may proceed concurrently with H3.11.5 Rosehip Cottage and later world
work because the normal SH1 change surface is confined to Story House content, generated-asset
manifests and Story House documentation.

The default content path is:

**select existing Story House text → define visual brief → generate image(s) in ChatGPT → stage in
Google Drive → R4 GitHub Actions materialisation → wire generated WebP to stable reader block IDs →
Cloudflare visual review → merge**

SH1 does not require the main game-development stream to stop.

## Concurrency contract

SH1 is parallel-safe with the world/interior stream when a title changes only:

- `public/stories/<story-id>/**`;
- `generated-assets/manifests/story-*.json`;
- Story House-specific content documentation;
- `public/stories/catalogue.json`, regenerated from story manifests.

Normal SH1 title work must not modify:

- exploration/world maps;
- NPC movement or dialogue systems;
- player movement/input;
- shop/interior gameplay;
- general UI outside Story House;
- save architecture;
- shared performance infrastructure.

If a title exposes a genuine Story House reader defect, isolate that runtime correction in its own
small foundation/remediation PR before continuing art production. Do not mix a broad reader rewrite
into an illustration-content PR.

Only one SH1 title/batch PR should update `catalogue.json` at a time. SH1 can run concurrently with
the main game stream, but multiple overlapping Story House catalogue PRs should be avoided unless
there is a specific reason.

## Illustration/provenance rules

Generated illustrations are new Story House assets, not replacements for public-domain source
files at repository level.

- Generated assets live under
  `public/stories/<story-id>/illustrations/generated/`.
- Historic/public-domain/source images remain in their existing source folders.
- Story manifests must describe generated-image provenance separately from historic illustration
  provenance.
- Public-domain text status and generated-image rights/provenance are separate metadata concerns.
- Every reader image attaches to a stable `blockId`; never use line numbers or character offsets.
- Generated images should be materialised through R4 rather than uploaded directly through the
  GitHub connector.
- Each title gets human visual review in Cloudflare before merge.

## Visual continuity contract

Before generating a multi-image title, establish a small title-specific visual bible containing:

- character appearance and distinguishing features;
- age/scale relationships;
- clothing/accessories that must remain stable;
- key location palette/materials;
- intended illustration medium/style;
- aspect ratio and reader framing;
- recurring props;
- elements that may deliberately change across the story.

For recurring characters, approve a small anchor set before generating the full book. Do not
generate twenty unrelated images and attempt to repair continuity afterwards.

The visual bible is content guidance, not a requirement to train/fine-tune a model.

### SH1.6.1 - Alice's Adventures in Wonderland modern illustration programme

**Approved scope (3 October 2026): 37 new generated assets: 36 Story House reader illustrations +
1 dedicated modern cover.**

Working branch:

- `agent/story-house-alice-modern-illustrations`

The Alice work is one coherent title package and remains on one feature branch until final approval.
Do **not** merge each image batch independently to `main`. Instead, complete and human-approve each
bounded batch, commit that approved checkpoint to the feature branch, then continue. The final Alice
PR is merged only after SH1.6.1F passes integration and visual review.

#### Visual direction

Alice should be a richly illustrated, whimsical Story House production rather than receiving the
lighter 3-5-image treatment used by short classics.

Overall art direction:

- painterly, richly detailed storybook illustration;
- colourful and inviting with controlled surrealism rather than flat cartoon rendering;
- Victorian-inspired architecture, costumes, furniture and props;
- humorous and peculiar rather than frightening;
- strong environmental depth and readable visual storytelling;
- avoid the distinctive visual language, costumes or character designs of later copyrighted film,
  television or animation adaptations;
- use Lewis Carroll / Tenniel-era concepts only as public-domain narrative/reference foundations,
  while making the generated visual treatment clearly its own Story House interpretation;
- early Wonderland scenes should remain relatively earthy and believable, becoming progressively
  more colourful, geometrically strange and theatrical as Alice travels further in;
- the Hearts court should feel like the visual peak: elaborate, absurd, crowded and slightly
  overwhelming without becoming sinister.

#### Alice continuity lock

Alice is the primary continuity anchor across the complete 36-image reader set.

Approved design intent to establish in SH1.6.1A:

- approximately 8-10 years old;
- fair/blonde hair, with one fixed hairstyle used throughout;
- pale blue Victorian-style dress;
- white pinafore/apron;
- dark shoes and stockings;
- expressive, curious and adventurous rather than permanently surprised;
- the same face, hair, costume details and body proportions in every scene;
- size changes alter Alice's scale relative to the environment, **not** her age, costume, face or
  proportions.

Once an Alice anchor image is approved, every later batch must use the approved Alice imagery as
the visual authority. Written prompt descriptions alone are not sufficient when an approved visual
reference exists.

#### Recurring-character visual bible

SH1.6.1A must also establish approved anchor designs for:

- White Rabbit: white fur, pink/red eyes, waistcoat, pocket watch, anxious posture;
- Caterpillar: blue, composed, slightly aloof, visually readable on the mushroom without becoming
  grotesque;
- Cheshire Cat: one stable coat pattern/colour, broad distinctive grin, visually capable of partial
  disappearance while remaining recognisably the same Cat;
- Hatter: eccentric Victorian clothing, fixed hat/costume and face;
- March Hare: stable fur, clothing/accessories if used, energetic body language;
- Dormouse: much smaller scale, sleepy and soft-featured;
- Queen of Hearts: theatrical Hearts royalty, strong silhouette and consistent dress/crown;
- King of Hearts: visually related Hearts court costume but clearly subordinate/less imposing;
- Gryphon: fixed hybrid anatomy and colour treatment across chapters 9-10;
- Mock Turtle: fixed creature anatomy, melancholy expression and palette across chapters 9-10.

Secondary one-scene characters may be established inside their first approved scene, but must remain
stable if they recur later.

#### Recurring-location visual bible

Lock the following environments before or during their first approved appearance:

- riverbank / ordinary-world opening;
- long door hall and tiny garden door;
- beautiful Wonderland garden;
- Wonderland woodland;
- White Rabbit's house;
- Caterpillar mushroom clearing;
- Duchess house / pepper kitchen;
- March Hare tea-party clearing;
- royal rose garden / croquet ground;
- Mock Turtle coastal setting;
- Hearts courtroom.

Recurring architecture, major props, palette and spatial identity should remain recognisable when a
location reappears.

## Per-title delivery loop

Each illustrated classic follows this small reviewable loop:

1. select title and confirm illustration scope;
2. produce/approve the visual bible or anchor images;
3. generate one bounded image batch;
4. stage images in Drive;
5. add/update R4 manifests;
6. let Actions materialise WebPs into the title branch;
7. wire assets to stable Story House blocks;
8. run focused catalogue/reader validation;
9. deploy Cloudflare preview;
10. human visual review;
11. iterate via same-Drive-file replacement where needed;
12. merge the title only after approval.

This loop is deliberately independent of H3.11.5+ world development.
