# Unicorn Valley - World, Characters and Content

## World premise

Unicorn Valley is a peaceful magical place made up of distinct storybook regions connected by paths, bridges and hidden routes.

The player is a new resident arriving at **Moonflower Cottage**, a small home on the edge of **Sunbeam Village**. There is no chosen-one prophecy and no world-ending threat. The story begins because the player has somewhere new to live and a whole valley to discover.

The valley's magic is expressed through small environmental wonders rather than complex lore exposition: glowing flowers, rainbows caught in puddles, singing crystals, curious creatures, moonlit paths and objects that behave in slightly impossible ways.

## World design principles

### Each region needs a strong identity

A child should be able to recognise where she is from colour, scenery and music without reading a location label.

### Areas should contain visible promises

The player should frequently see something she cannot yet fully reach or understand:

- a path behind a waterfall;
- a locked gate with stars on it;
- a bridge being repaired;
- a distant floating island;
- a glowing cave entrance;
- an unusual creature that runs away;
- a cloud path that obviously requires wings.

These create long-term curiosity without a conventional level screen.

### Backtracking must feel rewarding

Returning to an area should reveal:

- changed scenery;
- NPCs in new places;
- friendship-specific dialogue;
- newly accessible interactions;
- discoveries that depend on world state;
- event decorations;
- later hidden routes.

## Initial world map

The launch-sized game does not need every planned region. The architecture should nevertheless reserve clear expansion points.

### 1. Moonflower Glade

Purpose:

- player's home;
- safe starting area;
- first movement tutorial;
- first collectable discoveries;
- cottage decorating;
- later garden and companion systems.

Visual identity:

- soft grass;
- giant moonflowers;
- small stream;
- fireflies at dusk;
- curved stone path;
- cosy cottage with rounded architecture.

Notable locations:

- Moonflower Cottage;
- garden plot;
- little bridge;
- display stump/shelf for outdoor discoveries;
- initially mysterious hollow tree.

### 2. Sunbeam Village

Purpose:

- main social hub;
- shops;
- recurring NPCs;
- notice/suggestion board;
- access to surrounding regions.

Visual identity:

- warm colours;
- bunting;
- flowers around doorways;
- rounded colourful buildings;
- central fountain or magical sundial;
- visible signs using icons as well as text.

Notable locations:

- bakery;
- accessory shop;
- small library/story house;
- village square;
- bridge to Rainbow Meadow;
- path to Whispering Woods.

### 3. Rainbow Meadow

Purpose:

- open exploration;
- flower collecting;
- early secrets;
- racecourse access;
- picnic/event space.

Visual identity:

- broad bright meadow;
- moving clouds;
- rainbow fragments;
- tall grass patches;
- butterflies;
- oversized flowers.

Notable locations:

- Rainbow Run racecourse;
- picnic hill;
- old windmill or decorative tower;
- small pond;
- hidden flower circle.

### 4. Rainbow Run

Purpose:

- racing hub;
- Nova's main location;
- race registration;
- ribbon display;
- later championship events.

Visual identity:

- colourful banners;
- carved wooden starting arch;
- viewing hill;
- trophy/ribbon boards;
- glimpses of race tracks in the distance.

The first actual race can load into a dedicated race scene rather than requiring the full course to exist physically in the overworld.

### 5. Crystal Brook

Purpose:

- early exploration extension;
- shell/crystal discoveries;
- water-themed quests;
- later creature encounters.

Visual identity:

- bright shallow water;
- stepping stones;
- translucent crystals;
- willow-like trees;
- sparkling reflections.

This area can be introduced shortly after the initial vertical slice.

### 6. Whispering Woods

Purpose:

- gentle mystery;
- hidden paths;
- unusual plants;
- treasure hunts;
- night-time visual events.

Visual identity:

- deep blue-green canopy;
- glowing mushrooms;
- shafts of light;
- moving leaves;
- friendly rather than frightening ambience.

The woods should feel mysterious, not dark or threatening.

## Later expansion regions

### Starlight Beach

Shell collecting, tide pools, sand decorations, beach races and sea-creature discoveries.

### Cloudtop Peaks

Unlocked after flight becomes available. Floating paths, cloud jumping, high-altitude races and a major sense of progression.

### Lantern Marsh

A luminous wetland with frogs, fireflies, lantern plants and simple navigation puzzles. Must remain whimsical rather than spooky.

### Frostbell Vale

Snow, bells, ice slides and winter festival content. This may function as either a permanent region or seasonal-style content that remains manually accessible.

## Player's role in the world

The player is neither mayor nor saviour. She is a resident whose curiosity and friendships gradually make the valley richer.

Her actions can visibly improve or alter small parts of the world:

- helping create a garden;
- finding decorations for a festival;
- repairing a sign;
- helping an NPC gain confidence;
- discovering a hidden route;
- returning a lost object;
- helping hatch a creature;
- winning a race ribbon that is then displayed publicly.

The cumulative effect should make a long-running save look increasingly personal.

## Initial recurring characters

Names and details are working canon and can evolve during visual development.

### Nova

Role: racer and energetic friend.

Personality:

- confident;
- enthusiastic;
- competitive without being mean;
- occasionally nervous about disappointing herself.

Gameplay role:

- introduces racing;
- provides race challenges;
- teaches that losing is normal;
- later friendship arc around trying something she is not immediately good at.

Visual motif:

- bold star shapes;
- windswept mane;
- bright contrasting colours.

### Willow

Role: gardener and quiet observer.

Personality:

- gentle;
- thoughtful;
- notices small changes in nature;
- a little shy in groups.

Gameplay role:

- flower collecting;
- early friendship quest;
- gardening introduction later;
- demonstrates persistent world change through her garden.

A key early example: the player finds Moonflowers for Willow; after the quest, those Moonflowers visibly remain in Willow's garden.

### Pip

Role: curious explorer.

Personality:

- excitable;
- easily distracted;
- fascinated by mysteries;
- brave mainly because curiosity wins.

Gameplay role:

- secrets;
- treasure hunts;
- Wonderbook introduction;
- hidden paths;
- mysterious egg/creature storyline.

### Pebble

Role: collector and maker.

Personality:

- practical;
- cheerful;
- loves odd objects;
- sees potential uses for things others ignore.

Gameplay role:

- collecting systems;
- display shelves;
- simple craft-like interactions later;
- bridge/sign repairs;
- turns discoveries into decorations.

### Lumi

Role: keeper of stories and strange valley knowledge.

Personality:

- calm;
- playful;
- occasionally deliberately mysterious;
- never lectures when a simple hint will do.

Gameplay role:

- Wonderbook lore pages;
- clues to unusual discoveries;
- introduces magical world states;
- can hint at expansion areas without forcing progression.

### Marigold

Role: baker and event organiser.

Personality:

- sociable;
- slightly chaotic;
- generous;
- always preparing something.

Gameplay role:

- village events;
- baking mini-games later;
- picnic/festival quests;
- decorative food and friendship scenes.

## Character writing rules

Each main NPC should have:

- a recognisable silhouette;
- a clear visual motif;
- a personality that can be understood in a few interactions;
- interests that create gameplay opportunities;
- at least one insecurity or limitation so they do not feel one-note;
- visible changes as friendship progresses.

Avoid making each character a single moral lesson.

Dialogue should sound like characters talking, not adults writing educational content for children.

## Introductory story sequence

The opening should be brief and playable quickly.

Suggested sequence:

1. Title screen.
2. Create unicorn.
3. A short illustrated arrival at Moonflower Cottage.
4. Pip arrives to welcome the player.
5. Movement is taught by following Pip a short distance.
6. Player finds a sparkle/collectable on the route.
7. Pip gives the Wonderbook.
8. Player reaches Sunbeam Village.
9. Several characters are visible doing their own activities.
10. The player is given a choice of what to explore first.

The opening must not force the player through every major system before allowing free play.

## Initial story threads

### Thread A - Willow's Moonflowers

Purpose:

- teach talking;
- teach simple collecting;
- demonstrate world persistence.

Flow:

1. Willow mentions that her Moonflowers have not grown well.
2. Player notices wild Moonflowers near the glade/river.
3. Collect a small number.
4. Return to Willow.
5. Willow plants them.
6. Garden changes immediately or after a simple scene transition.
7. Later dialogue references the flowers.

Reward:

- flower-themed accessory or cottage decoration;
- Wonderbook memory.

### Thread B - Nova's First Race

Purpose:

- introduce racing;
- make participation rewarding.

Flow:

1. Meet Nova near Rainbow Run.
2. She invites the player to a practice race.
3. Short tutorial race.
4. Result-specific friendly dialogue.
5. Player receives first ribbon regardless of position.
6. A later named race becomes available.

### Thread C - Pip and the Strange Egg

Purpose:

- establish long-term curiosity and return visits.

Flow:

1. Pip notices unusual tracks or sparkles.
2. Player follows a short clue trail.
3. A strange egg is discovered.
4. The egg can be kept at Moonflower Cottage.
5. Several unrelated play activities can advance a non-real-time progression flag.
6. The egg changes visually.
7. Eventually it hatches into a companion creature.

This should not use a real-world multi-day timer in the first implementation. Progress is based on game activity/session milestones so the player is never told to wait 24 hours.

### Thread D - Marigold's Picnic Problem

Purpose:

- bring several NPCs together;
- introduce decorative choices.

Flow:

1. Marigold wants to organise a meadow picnic.
2. Player chooses between several decoration themes or helps find missing items.
3. NPCs gather in the meadow.
4. Picnic remains as a temporary or persistent visual event.
5. NPC dialogue reflects the chosen decorations.

## World-state examples

The following types of persistent state should be supported from the beginning:

- `willowMoonflowersPlanted`
- `firstRaceCompleted`
- `rainbowCupWon`
- `strangeEggFound`
- `strangeEggStage`
- `picnicTheme`
- `bridgeRepaired`
- `secretGroveDiscovered`
- `novaFriendshipTier`

The exact implementation is technical, but content should be authored with these stateful consequences in mind.

## Secrets philosophy

Secrets should be discoverable through curiosity rather than obscure internet-guide logic.

Good secrets:

- walk behind a waterfall;
- follow an unusual butterfly;
- return to a flower circle at night;
- wear a friendship accessory while talking to the character who gave it;
- notice a sparkle partially hidden by scenery;
- investigate a sound;
- revisit a repaired location.

Bad secrets:

- 0.1% random chance;
- perform ten unexplained actions in an exact order;
- content permanently missable after one opportunity.

## Event philosophy

Events create concentrated moments of excitement without using real-world FOMO.

Potential events:

- Rainbow Cup;
- Moonflower Night;
- Sunbeam Picnic;
- Starlight Beach Day;
- Lantern Parade;
- Frostbell Festival.

In early versions events should be triggered by game progress or selectable from the world rather than disappearing because a calendar date passed.

## Content authoring target

A good content unit is small enough to implement and test independently.

Examples:

- one NPC conversation chain;
- one three-step quest;
- one secret;
- one new collectable set;
- one race course;
- one cottage furniture set;
- one persistent world change;
- one Wonderbook page.

This allows future work to add depth without destabilising the entire game.
