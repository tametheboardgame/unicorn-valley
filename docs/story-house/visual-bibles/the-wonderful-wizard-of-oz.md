# The Wonderful Wizard of Oz — modern Story House visual bible

Work package: **R6.5-WP19SH1.5.1**

## Purpose

Create one coherent ten-image modern illustration set for **The Wonderful Wizard of Oz** and make it
independently selectable against both the Story House retelling and the Full Classic Text.

The existing W. W. Denslow files remain the **Classic Illustrations** option. The generated set
becomes **Modern Illustrations**.

This is the first long-form SH1 title. The main quality bar is sustained continuity across Dorothy,
Toto, the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman and Cowardly Lion over the entire journey without drifting into
well-known film designs.

## Source-design guardrail

Use the public-domain Baum novel as the story reference, not later film adaptations.

- Dorothy wears **silver shoes**, never ruby slippers.
- Avoid distinctive costumes, makeup, silhouettes or production design associated with famous film
  versions.
- The Emerald City, Witch, Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, Lion and Glinda should be original Story House
  interpretations grounded in Baum's text.
- Do not make the Wicked Witch green-skinned by default; the novel's important visual cue is her
  single powerful eye.
- Oz is a small ordinary older man behind theatrical tricks, not a film-specific likeness.

## Visual direction

- warm hand-painted children's storybook illustration;
- soft gouache / watercolour texture with expressive light and rich natural colour;
- whimsical but not glossy CGI;
- clear shift from muted Kansas to increasingly saturated Oz;
- environments remain believable enough that the characters feel they are walking through one
  continuous world;
- no baked-in typography, captions, logos, borders or UI.

## Recurring character anchors

### Dorothy

- child around ten to twelve years old;
- medium-brown hair in two loose braids tied with simple blue ribbons;
- practical blue-and-white checked cotton dress over a cream blouse;
- silver shoes after arriving in Oz;
- small wicker basket when travelling;
- curious, determined expression rather than princess styling;
- same face, hair and dress silhouette in every Oz scene.

### Toto

- small black terrier-like dog;
- wiry coat, alert ears and bright dark eyes;
- compact enough for Dorothy to pick up easily;
- no clothing apart from a simple narrow collar.

### Scarecrow

- friendly painted cloth face, visibly stuffed with straw;
- faded blue shirt, patched tan trousers and soft pointed brown hat;
- rope ties at wrists/ankles;
- lanky, loose-limbed silhouette;
- expressive but never human-skinned.

### Tin Woodman

- brushed pewter / pale tin body with visible rivets and practical jointing;
- simple funnel-like cap integrated into an original silhouette;
- broad friendly face made from shaped metal, not a human face painted silver;
- woodman's axe carried safely;
- oil can can recur as a prop.

### Cowardly Lion

- large naturalistic tawny lion;
- warm golden-brown mane;
- expressive eyes and ears;
- physically powerful but emotionally readable without cartoon clothes or accessories.

### Wicked Witch of the West

- older woman with weathered face and one dominant, unnaturally sharp eye;
- dark layered travelling robes in charcoal, indigo and muted plum;
- golden cap is a key recurring prop;
- imposing through posture and gaze, not grotesque makeup.

### Oz

- small older man;
- balding grey hair, neat moustache, practical waistcoat and rolled shirt sleeves;
- stagecraft / machinery around him should make the deception readable;
- ordinary and slightly nervous rather than sinister.

### Glinda

- young-looking adult woman;
- rich red hair in flowing curls;
- white gown with restrained ruby accents;
- warm, intelligent expression;
- elegant but distinct from any screen adaptation.

## Environment continuity

### Kansas

- flat grey-brown prairie;
- weathered timber farmhouse;
- muted sky and restrained colour palette.

### Munchkin Country

- sudden colour contrast: blue-painted cottages, green fields, bright flowers;
- yellow brick road begins clearly;
- architecture feels handmade and rural rather than theme-park-like.

### Yellow Brick Road / forest

- same brick colour and construction throughout;
- fields transition naturally into deeper forest;
- keep road scale consistent under the group.

### Emerald City

- green marble, green glass and emerald-like highlights;
- luminous but still architectural;
- visitors wear green spectacles at the gate;
- avoid reproducing iconic film city silhouettes.

### Western country

- rougher, drier terrain and darker skies;
- Witch's castle is practical stone fortress architecture, not gothic horror.

### Glinda's south

- warmer red/rose accents, polished pale stone and cultivated gardens;
- feels distinct from Emerald City while still part of Oz.

## Bounded generation plan

Do not generate all ten independently in one uncontrolled pass.

### Batch A — core character anchors

1. Dorothy arrives in Oz
2. Meeting the Scarecrow
3. The Tin Woodman
4. The Lion joins the road

Review these four together before proceeding. Dorothy/Toto are anchored in image 1; each travelling
companion is then introduced once.

### Batch B — world and conflict continuity

5. Emerald City
6. The Witch watches
7. Oz behind the screen

Use the approved Batch A character appearances unchanged. The Emerald City image is the first
full-group checkpoint.

### Batch C — resolution

8. Gifts from Oz
9. Glinda the Good
10. Home to Kansas

Keep all approved appearances stable. The final Kansas scene should echo image 1 compositionally
without importing Oz colour into the prairie.

## Long-form continuity rules

- Dorothy, Toto, Scarecrow, Tin Woodman and Lion must be recognisable across every appearance.
- Never change Dorothy's silver shoes to red.
- Scarecrow patches, hat silhouette and shirt colour remain fixed.
- Tin Woodman's body construction and face design remain fixed.
- Lion mane shape/colour remains fixed.
- Do not overpopulate scenes merely because the full novel contains many side characters.
- Prefer one strong scene per Story House section to excessive illustration density.
- Full Classic Text reuses the same generated files at matching stable block IDs.

## Independent text / illustration contract

### Story House Edition

- existing Denslow mapping becomes `classic`;
- generated ten-image set becomes `modern`;
- default illustration set: `modern`.

### Full Classic Text

- existing Denslow mapping becomes `classic`;
- same generated files reused against matching Full Classic page/block anchors;
- default illustration set: `classic`.

The reader must continue to allow all four combinations:

1. Story House + Classic Illustrations;
2. Story House + Modern Illustrations;
3. Full Classic Text + Classic Illustrations;
4. Full Classic Text + Modern Illustrations.

## Human gates

- Gate A: approve Batch A character anchors before Batch B.
- Gate B: approve Emerald City full-group continuity before completing Batch C.
- Final gate: review all ten images together in Story House and sample Full Classic Text + Modern
  Illustrations before merge.
