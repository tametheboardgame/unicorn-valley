# The Tale of Jemima Puddle-Duck — modern illustration visual bible

Work package: R6.5-WP19SH1.6.3A

## Audit baseline

The Story House Edition has five chapters and **11 stable content blocks**. It currently uses four
historic Beatrix Potter illustrations. The separate Full Classic Text has 10 reader pages and all
25 historic Potter narrative illustrations.

The SH1.6.3 modernisation must:

- leave the Full Classic Text and all 25 historic illustrations unchanged;
- preserve the four existing Story House Potter images as the selectable Classic Illustrations set;
- add a separate original Modern Illustrations set to the Story House Edition;
- attach each modern image to an existing stable block ID;
- select the modern catalogue cover later from the approved reader art under the canonical Story
  House cover rule rather than generating a cover-only asset.

## Art direction

Use the established modern Unicorn Valley Story House language: rich hand-painted storybook art,
painterly and cinematic without looking photographic or 3D-rendered. The setting should feel like
an English country farm and spring woodland in the early twentieth century, but compositions,
character designs and staging must be original.

Do **not** imitate Beatrix Potter's illustration style or reconstruct historic Potter compositions.
The public-domain story can be depicted, but the modern set needs its own visual identity.

Reader images should favour clear landscape compositions with strong silhouettes and readable
action at small sizes. Keep important faces/subjects reasonably centre-safe so an approved reader
image can also work as the modern catalogue cover if its crop is suitable.

No typography, speech bubbles, captions, logos, decorative borders or title text may be baked into
the artwork.

## Character anchors

### Jemima Puddle-Duck

- Adult white domestic duck with clean white plumage and an orange bill and feet.
- Blue bonnet is her strongest visual identifier and must remain consistent.
- Patterned shawl in a complementary muted country palette; keep the same pattern, shape and drape
  throughout the book.
- Natural duck anatomy and proportions, with expressive head/neck/body language rather than human
  hands or a human body shape.
- Determined, independent and slightly trusting rather than foolish-looking.
- When flying, retain recognisable bonnet/shawl continuity without turning the clothing into a
  visual obstruction.

### Sandy-whiskered gentleman / fox

- Adult red/sandy fox, larger than Jemima but still naturalistic in animal proportions.
- Pointed dark/black ears, sandy whiskers, long full bushy tail and pale throat/chest.
- Intelligent, controlled expression. Early scenes should read as charming and polite; later scenes
  become impatient and predatory without horror, gore or exaggerated villain features.
- The folded/open newspaper is an important recurring prop in the first meeting.
- Default to no formal human outfit. His "gentleman" quality comes from posture, manners and the
  newspaper unless human review explicitly approves clothing during the anchor stage.

### Kep

- Working farm collie, black-and-white coat, athletic build and alert ears.
- Larger than Jemima, protective but not intimidating.
- Intelligent, observant expression; his visual language should contrast with the fox's polished
  charm by feeling practical, direct and trustworthy.
- No collar ornament, clothes or fantasy accessories unless already supported by the existing game
  world art direction.

### Supporting animals/people

- The farmer's wife may appear in the opening farm scene in simple period-appropriate rural
  clothing. She does not require a recurring character anchor.
- The two dogs who help Kep in the rescue are supporting farm dogs, clearly distinct from Kep but
  do not need independent recurring designs.

## Location anchors

### Farm and cart road

- Lived-in English farmyard with stone or limewashed farmhouse, timber barn/outbuildings, haystack,
  kitchen/herb garden and a clear cart road leading towards the hill/wood.
- Warm spring light, practical textures and gentle signs of farm activity.
- Keep the route towards the wood geographically legible across Chapters 1, 4 and 5.

### Woodland clearing and feather shed

- Sun-dappled mixed woodland with ferns, spring ground plants and a small clearing.
- A tree stump near the meeting point for the fox and his newspaper.
- Small tumble-down timber shed set just behind/within the clearing.
- The shed interior contains an unusually deep layer of loose feathers and later Jemima's nest.
  The feathers are a warning clue, not a gruesome image.
- Keep stump, shed door, clearing edge and path orientation stable so images 3, 4, 5, 9 and 10 read
  as the same place.

## Agreed modern reader illustration plan — 11 images

### 1. The Farmyard Problem — `a-farmyard-problem`

Jemima stands beside another too-easily-discovered farm nest, visibly frustrated but determined,
while the farmer's wife gently attends to the eggs nearby. Establish Jemima's final bonnet/shawl
design, her scale and the warm working farm environment.

This is the primary Jemima character/farm continuity scene.

### 2. Off to the Secret Wood — `a-quiet-nest`

A wider, airy composition of Jemima taking flight from the hill/cart road and skimming towards the
woodland clearing, with the farm receding behind her. Her flying should feel slightly awkward but
successful, matching the prose.

This is a potential modern-cover candidate if the final crop keeps Jemima readable.

### 3. The Sandy-whiskered Gentleman — `the-sandy-whiskered-gentleman`

Jemima encounters the fox beside the tree stump. He lowers or peers over his newspaper with
perfectly polite interest while Jemima approaches openly. The image should feel charming on first
look but contain a small amount of visual unease.

This is the primary fox continuity anchor and another potential cover candidate.

### 4. The Feather Shed — `the-feather-shed`

The fox opens the tumble-down shed and Jemima looks approvingly at the soft nest area while the
deep layer of feathers provides the first strong warning clue. The fox remains outwardly helpful.

Match the clearing/shed geography established in image 3.

### 5. Nine Eggs — `nine-eggs`

Jemima sits proudly beside her nine large greenish-white eggs in the completed nest. The fox counts
or watches the eggs from the doorway/background with an interest that is just slightly too intense.

Keep the nest, shed interior and egg count unambiguous.

### 6. The Dinner Invitation — `a-dinner-invitation`

The fox politely explains his "dinner" idea while Jemima listens with mild puzzlement. Visually
suggest the herbs/onions request through gesture and context, but do not include a readable written
shopping list or any baked-in text.

The scene should be funny enough for a child to recognise the suspicious request without making the
threat graphic.

### 7. Kep Notices — `kep-notices`

Back on the farm, Jemima carries the gathered herbs and two onions while Kep stops and studies the
unusual bundle. Jemima is matter-of-fact; Kep is immediately alert.

This is the primary Kep continuity anchor and re-establishes the farm geography.

### 8. The Whole Story — `the-whole-story`

Jemima explains where the nest is and who helped her. Kep's expression/body language changes from
curiosity to concern as he understands the situation. The direction of the wood/cart road should
be clear so his decision to run for help has a visual destination.

Keep the herb/onion bundle consistent with image 7.

### 9. Something Is Wrong — `something-is-wrong`

Jemima returns to the clearing. The fox is waiting without his relaxed newspaper pose and appears
impatient near the open shed. Feathers are visible again. Jemima has stopped and taken one cautious
step backwards as the clues finally connect.

Shift the light slightly cooler/more enclosed than the earlier woodland scenes, while remaining
appropriate for a young reader.

### 10. Kep to the Rescue — `back-to-safety`

Kep reaches Jemima with two other farm dogs as the fox retreats into the trees. Stage the image so
Jemima and Kep are clearly safe in the foreground/midground while the other dogs drive the fox away
in the background.

The disturbed nest can be visible, but avoid graphic broken eggs, injury or an animal attack.
Emphasise relief and Kep staying close to Jemima after the danger passes.

### 11. Four Ducklings — `a-second-chance`

Warm summer closing scene near the farm: Jemima rests proudly beside a safe nest/yard area with her
four newly hatched ducklings. Kep may be nearby as a friendly continuity callback.

This is the emotional resolution: safe, warm, independent and hopeful. It is also a strong modern
cover candidate if the catalogue crop keeps Jemima and at least some ducklings readable.

## Deliberate omissions

- Do not attempt to recreate or modernise all 25 historic Potter images one-for-one.
- No separate image solely for the farmer's wife finding each earlier nest.
- No gruesome implication for the feathers in the shed.
- No explicit depiction of the fox attacking Jemima or the dogs attacking the fox.
- No graphic broken eggs in the rescue aftermath.
- No dedicated modern-cover generation during the reader-art phase; choose from approved reader art
  during integration.

## Visual progression

Warm busy farm → airy independent journey → charming sun-dappled woodland → increasingly suspicious
shed → warm farm warning/intervention → cooler woodland realisation → energetic rescue → warm summer
home and ducklings.

The story should become tense through composition and lighting, not through horror imagery.
