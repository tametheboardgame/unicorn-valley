## Art direction

Use the established modern Story House language: rich painterly cinematic storybook art, readable
at small reader sizes, expressive but not hyper-real, no baked-in captions or title typography.

The woods should feel mysterious and responsive rather than threatening. Tension comes from
disorientation, quiet and distance from home, not horror imagery.

Colour progression:

**warm gold village → green-gold woods → cool silver clearing → multicolour lantern climax → warm
gold homecoming**

Reader images are landscape, targeting 1536 × 1024. Keep important subjects reasonably centre-safe
because the final modern catalogue cover will reuse one approved reader image.

## Character continuity

### Rowan — new canonical design to approve

Rowan has no existing game implementation, so SH1.8A establishes him.

- young unicorn; visibly juvenile proportions, smaller than adult village residents;
- warm chestnut/russet body with a softly lighter muzzle;
- moss-green eyes;
- short, slightly tousled deep-auburn mane;
- matching auburn tail with a natural soft plume;
- small pale-gold horn;
- no clothes or permanent carried accessory;
- curious, earnest and slightly impatient at the start;
- expression/body language becomes calmer and more observant through the woods sequence.

Do not age him into an adult horse-like build. Keep the face readable and childlike without
becoming chibi.

### Quill — existing game continuity, not a redesign

The game implementation is authoritative:

- sky-blue body;
- green eyes;
- swept midnight mane;
- lilac plume tail;
- moon-style horn;
- star marking;
- no accessory.

Quill is the Story House librarian/storykeeper. In the story scene Quill may hold the established
small blue book/silver bookmark motif, but this is a scene prop rather than a permanent costume
change.

### Chapter-4 supporting villagers

The baker, gardener and two racing foals are secondary ensemble characters. They must stay visually
subordinate to Rowan and Quill. Do not create new named-character canon during SH1.8A. If existing
game residents are deliberately reused later, that mapping must be made explicitly rather than by
accident.

## Environment continuity

### Hill village and edge lantern

Treat the story village as a painterly Story House interpretation of the existing bright Valley
village language rather than a literal game-map screenshot.

Recurring cues:

- small cottages climbing a gentle hill;
- bakery near Rowan's opening viewpoint;
- low garden walls and planted verges;
- one winding path leading beyond the final cottage to the woods;
- warm evening lanterns progressively lit from the village fountain/outward direction.

The **edge lantern** is the recurring title object:

- taller than the later clearing lantern;
- weathered dark-brass/iron post and frame;
- four cloudy glass panes;
- small peaked cap;
- initially no flame;
- responds with a faint cool silver glow before eventually becoming warm gold.

Keep this design stable in images 1, 3, 11 and 12.

### Moving woods, pale path and glowmoths

- mixed broadleaf woodland;
- dense ferns, soft moss, roots and occasional fallen logs;
- pale flat stones forming the readable path;
- geography feels subtly unreliable through framing/path curvature, not impossible Escher geometry;
- never horror-dark.

**Glowmoths** are actual small moths, not fireflies or floating magic orbs:

- delicate visible wings;
- green-gold bioluminescent glow concentrated through wings/body;
- one lead moth can read clearly in close compositions;
- groups form a soft dotted path-light rather than a neon swarm.

### Quiet clearing and mirror lantern

- round woodland clearing with the sky just visible through the canopy;
- calmer, cooler and more silver-blue than the path;
- ancient lantern in the centre, smaller and older than the village-edge lantern;
- low moss-covered stone/metal base;
- cloudy glass;
- small hinged access door;
- no candle;
- one simple mirror mounted inside at a stable angle;
- glowmoths can settle on the lantern roof.

The reflected-light mechanism must remain visually understandable: distant/nearby lantern light
enters via the mirror and is redistributed through the cloudy glass. Avoid turning it into an
unexplained magical energy beam.

## Agreed reader illustration plan — 12 images

1. **The Village Lanterns** — `lantern-row`
   - Rowan on/near the bakery step watching warm lights appear through the hill village.
   - The final edge lantern beside the woods remains clearly dark.

2. **A Question for Quill** — `question-for-quill`
   - Rowan asks Quill about the unlit lantern.
   - Quill looks over a small blue book with the Story House/library atmosphere behind them.
   - Locks Rowan/Quill relative scale.

3. **The First Glimmer** — `first-glimmer`
   - Rowan beside the dark edge lantern at dusk.
   - One green-gold glowmoth blinks between two trunks.
   - The edge lantern answers with a faint silver glimmer as Rowan steps towards the wood.
   - Early cover candidate.

4. **The Path That Moved** — `moving-path`
   - pale stones through ferns beneath drifting glowmoths;
   - Rowan looks back and cannot see the village;
   - the forward path now appears to curve differently.

5. **Chasing the Light** — `chasing-light`
   - scratched/frustrated Rowan stops after chasing the brightest glowmoth;
   - the lead glowmoth stops too;
   - visual emphasis on the moment he finally becomes still.

6. **Walking at Their Pace** — `shared-pace`
   - several glowmoths settle along the pale stones;
   - Rowan walks calmly with them;
   - fork/fallen-log geography is readable;
   - first glimpse of the circular clearing ahead.

7. **The Quiet Clearing** — `quiet-clearing`
   - wide reveal of the round clearing and ancient central lantern;
   - Rowan opens the little door and discovers the mirror.

8. **Reflected Light** — `reflected-light`
   - Rowan sees himself, glowmoths and tiny distant village lights in the mirror;
   - reflected light enters the old lantern and begins to illuminate the cloudy glass.

9. **One Light Is Not Enough** — `one-light-not-enough`
   - dim clearing after Rowan's unsuccessful attempts;
   - Rowan sits beside the lantern while glowmoths gather on its roof;
   - realisation/solitude rather than action.

10. **Returning With Friends** — `return-with-friends`
    - Rowan leads the baker, gardener, two racing foals and Quill through the now-readable woods;
    - flour-dusted lantern, green glass lamp, two safely mounted saddlebag lamps and Quill's blue
      reading light provide distinct practical light sources.

11. **The Lantern Chain** — `lantern-chain`
    - visual climax;
    - several coloured lights reflect through the ancient mirror;
    - the clearing lantern shines like a captured star;
    - distant edge lantern answers and the path begins lighting lantern-by-lantern towards home.
    - strongest likely spectacle-cover candidate.

12. **The Shared Path** — `shared-path`
    - warm epilogue at the now-lit edge lantern;
    - Rowan walks with/supports another uncertain villager towards the beginning of the path;
    - friendship and companionship are the focus rather than another large spectacle.
