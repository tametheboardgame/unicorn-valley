# The Tale of Peter Rabbit - modern Story House visual bible

Work package: **R6.5-WP19SH1.6.2**

Status: **draft continuity lock; SH1.6.2A anchor review pending**

## Source audit

The Story House retelling is five chapters with 13 stable content blocks:

1. `four-little-rabbits`
2. `why-the-garden-was-forbidden`
3. `under-the-gate`
4. `among-the-vegetables`
5. `the-chase`
6. `the-gooseberry-net`
7. `the-watering-can`
8. `through-the-tools`
9. `asking-the-sparrows`
10. `finding-the-gate`
11. `back-in-the-burrow`
12. `a-quiet-evening`
13. `tomorrow`

The Full Classic Text is a separate ten-page edition with all **26 historic Beatrix Potter narrative
illustrations**. Those files, mappings and provenance are frozen and are not modern-generation
targets.

The current Story House edition has four historic Potter images. The new work adds an independently
selectable Modern Illustrations set rather than replacing the historic experience.

## Modern visual language

- rich, painterly, cinematic modern storybook art;
- dimensional natural materials, soft realistic fur and tactile garden detail;
- expressive but believable animal faces rather than cartoon exaggeration;
- warm woodland/burrow light contrasted with brighter garden daylight and cooler chase shadows;
- readable silhouettes and clear action at Story House reader scale;
- landscape-first compositions for reader illustrations;
- no imitation of Beatrix Potter's watercolour/ink treatment;
- no tracing, recreation or camera-copy of historic Potter compositions;
- no collage layouts, comic panels, baked-in text, logos, captions or UI;
- continuity takes priority over novelty once the anchor set is approved.

## Character continuity

### Peter Rabbit

- young European rabbit, clearly juvenile but not baby-proportioned;
- compact, agile build with realistic rabbit hindquarters and long expressive ears;
- warm medium-brown fur with slightly lighter cream muzzle, throat and underside;
- dark brown eyes with small natural catchlights;
- narrow face, soft cheeks and a small pink-brown nose;
- signature **deep cornflower-blue jacket**, simple late-Victorian rural cut, brass-toned buttons;
- no trousers;
- brown leather shoes only before the chase removes them;
- approximate standing height: 34 cm, keeping garden plants and human props visibly large around him;
- movement language: curious upright pauses, low rabbit crouch when listening, explosive bounding
  sprint when frightened;
- expressions remain subtle and animal-led: curiosity, guilty confidence, alarm, determination,
  exhaustion.

Continuity change points:

- Chapters 1-2: jacket + both shoes present.
- Early Chapter 3 chase: jacket + shoes initially present.
- After `the-chase`: shoes are gone.
- After `the-gooseberry-net`: jacket is gone.
- `the-watering-can` onward: no jacket, no shoes, fur increasingly wet/muddy.
- Home scenes: muddy/wet arrival, then dried and wrapped warmly in bed.

### Mrs Rabbit

- adult doe, visibly larger and calmer than the children;
- warm chestnut-brown fur with cream muzzle/chest;
- kind but firm face, longer mature proportions than Peter;
- practical moss-green dress with a muted plum shawl or wrap;
- woven market basket used in the opening and return-home continuity;
- posture and expression should communicate capable parent rather than comic fussiness.

### Flopsy, Mopsy and Cotton-tail

All three are Peter's siblings, similar juvenile scale but individually recognisable without relying
on labels.

- **Flopsy:** slightly darker brown fur, one ear naturally flops a little at the tip, dusty-rose
  neck ribbon.
- **Mopsy:** warm tawny fur, both ears upright, sage-green neck ribbon.
- **Cotton-tail:** lightest sandy-brown fur, bright white tail and cream forehead blaze, pale-gold
  neck ribbon.
- simple accessories only; no elaborate human clothing;
- together they should read as a family with shared proportions, not three unrelated rabbit designs.

### Mr McGregor

- late-middle-aged gardener, tall relative to Peter and physically imposing at rabbit scale;
- weathered fair-to-ruddy face, grey-brown hair and short rough beard/stubble;
- heavy brows and focused, irritated expression rather than villainous caricature;
- forest-green waistcoat over an off-white work shirt, rolled sleeves;
- brown work trousers, leather boots and worn gardening apron;
- recurring broad-brimmed weathered hat may be used outdoors;
- primary gardening tool: long wooden-handled hoe;
- hands, boots and tools should reinforce Peter's tiny scale.

## Environment continuity

### Rabbit home and fir-tree burrow

- entrance under exposed roots of a very large old fir tree;
- sandy earth bank, moss, ferns and bramble edges;
- warm burrow interior with rounded earth walls, roots crossing the ceiling and simple handmade
  wooden furniture;
- soft amber lamplight/firelight indoors;
- Mrs Rabbit's basket, crockery and simple bedding recur;
- opening and home-again scenes must clearly be the same place.

### Mr McGregor's garden

Treat the garden as one coherent geography across the book, not a different garden in every image.

Core layout:

- weathered timber gate in a thick mixed hedge;
- broad central path of pale compacted gravel/earth;
- ordered vegetable beds edged with dark soil;
- lettuces and beans nearest the entry sequence;
- radishes/carrots and cabbages further in;
- gooseberry bushes/netting on one side route;
- glass greenhouse visible as a recurring orientation landmark;
- timber tool shed on the far side of the garden;
- small pond with stone edge and cat territory near the shed-side route;
- currant bushes leading towards the return sightline to the gate.

Recurring props:

- wooden-handled hoe;
- watering can large enough for Peter to hide inside;
- terracotta flowerpots;
- seed trays, twine, netting and wooden crates;
- Peter's lost blue jacket later visible as an abandoned garden object if composition allows.

### Scale contract

- vegetables are large relative to Peter but not fantasy-giant;
- human doors, pots, tools and greenhouse fittings should feel oversized from rabbit eye level;
- Mr McGregor should dominate frames without becoming grotesquely giant;
- rabbit family scale must remain consistent between home and garden scenes.

## Colour and lighting progression

- **Home/opening:** warm honey/amber light, moss green, soft browns, calm family atmosphere.
- **Garden discovery:** fresh spring greens, warm sun, saturated vegetable colour, inviting wonder.
- **Chase:** higher contrast, cooler green shadows, flashes of warm sunlight, stronger motion.
- **Lost sequence:** slightly cooler and more enclosed, garden structures and tools looming larger.
- **Escape:** brighter gate/field light ahead to make the route home visually legible.
- **Home again:** warm amber returns, softer contrast, quiet evening blue outside the burrow.

## Modern scene inventory

Target: **12 reader illustrations**, deliberately not a one-for-one recreation of the 26 historic
classic images.

### SH1.6.2B - Chapters 1-2

1. **Family beneath the fir tree** - `four-little-rabbits`
   - Mrs Rabbit with all four children at the burrow entrance, basket ready.
   - Establishes Peter and family proportions, clothing and home environment.

2. **Peter watches the forbidden garden** - `why-the-garden-was-forbidden`
   - siblings leave down the lane while Peter looks through hedge gaps towards the distant gate.
   - Original suspense composition, not based on a historic Potter plate.

3. **Under the gate** - `under-the-gate`
   - low rabbit-eye view of Peter squeezing beneath the weathered garden gate into ordered beds.
   - Establishes the gate, hedge and garden geography.

4. **Too many vegetables** - `among-the-vegetables`
   - Peter among lettuces/radishes, visibly over-full, with Mr McGregor newly appearing at the far
     end of the row.
   - Establishes the human/rabbit scale relationship before the chase.

### SH1.6.2C - Chapters 3-5

5. **The chase begins** - `the-chase`
   - Peter sprinting between rows, Mr McGregor following with hoe, one shoe already lost in the
     foreground or mid-flight.

6. **Caught in the gooseberry net** - `the-gooseberry-net`
   - blue jacket snagged while Peter wriggles free, McGregor approaching through foliage.

7. **The watering-can sneeze** - `the-watering-can`
   - soaked Peter inside/launching from the oversized watering can in the tool shed.

8. **Lost among the tools** - `through-the-tools`
   - Peter moving beneath pots/tools with the pond cat visible but not attacking; greenhouse/shed
     landmarks maintain geography.

9. **The sparrows show the way** - `asking-the-sparrows`
   - sparrows lifting towards the visible gate beyond currant bushes; Peter follows their direction.

10. **Race for the gate** - `finding-the-gate`
    - low dynamic view of Peter reaching the gate first with bright field beyond and Mr McGregor
      behind.

11. **Back in the burrow** - `back-in-the-burrow`
    - muddy, wet, coatless Peter facing Mrs Rabbit just inside the same home established in image 1.

12. **Camomile and bed** - `a-quiet-evening`
    - clean/dried Peter tucked into bed with tea while his sisters have bread, milk and blackberries
      nearby; quiet warm ending.

`tomorrow` remains text-led unless later review shows the ending needs a separate image. Avoid
adding an extra image merely to illustrate every block.

## Anchor set for SH1.6.2A

These four images are continuity tests, not automatically final book assets.

### Anchor A - Peter Rabbit

A clean cinematic character/environment portrait showing Peter at natural rabbit scale beside low
garden foliage. Full body visible. Blue jacket and both shoes present. Neutral-curious expression.
This is the primary face, fur, ear, jacket and body-proportion lock.

### Anchor B - Rabbit family

Mrs Rabbit with Flopsy, Mopsy, Cotton-tail and Peter outside the fir-root burrow. The image must make
all five designs legible and prove that the siblings can be distinguished without over-costuming.

### Anchor C - Mr McGregor

Mr McGregor in the garden near a vegetable bed, full figure or three-quarter figure with hoe and
greenhouse/shed context. The goal is to lock face, clothing, body scale and gardener tone without
turning him into a comic villain.

### Anchor D - Garden environment

Wide establishing view from roughly rabbit-eye height showing the gate, central path, vegetable
beds, greenhouse, shed and hedge structure in one coherent garden. Peter may be omitted or very
small; the environment layout is the subject.
