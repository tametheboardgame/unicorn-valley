# R6.5 - Autonomous Unicorn Life

## Purpose

R6.5 should not make Unicorn Valley feel more populated merely by placing additional static NPCs around the maps.

A living valley requires visible movement. Unicorns should appear to have places to go, things to look at and routines that continue whether or not the player is speaking to them.

This document supplements `07V-R6.5-VALLEY-COMPLETENESS-BREADTH.md` and `07W-R6.5-CONTENT-BLUEPRINT.md`. Its requirements belong primarily to R6.5-WP2 and are then consumed by the region-depth packages.

The objective is **believable ambient life**, not a heavyweight life-simulation system.

---

# Core rule

A populated region must not look like a collection of unicorn statues.

Where navigation and story state allow it, a meaningful proportion of visible residents should be doing something other than standing permanently on one coordinate.

Movement may be simple. What matters is that it creates the impression that the valley continues around the player.

Examples:

- walking from one landmark to another;
- wandering around a safe garden, square, meadow or shoreline zone;
- trotting a short warm-up loop near Rainbow Run;
- moving from a shop doorway to a bench or display;
- pausing to look at a fountain, flowers, crystals or the sea;
- walking over to another unicorn and idling nearby for a while;
- appearing in a different sensible location after a scene transition or progression change;
- visiting Moonflower Glade after friendship/story milestones.

NPC movement should communicate ordinary life, not urgency.

---

# Behaviour types

The reusable resident system should support several lightweight routine types.

## 1. Local wander

The unicorn moves between safe points inside a bounded zone.

Examples:

- around Sunbeam Village square;
- around Willow's garden;
- beside the Crystal Brook shoreline;
- around a Starlight Beach tide-pool area.

Rules:

- movement remains inside authored walkable space;
- pauses are common so residents do not look restless;
- destination order can vary within safe limits;
- the resident never wanders through scenery, water, walls, race gates or transition triggers.

## 2. Purposeful route

The unicorn has a small authored circuit between recognisable places.

Examples:

- Bakery -> fountain -> bench -> Bakery;
- ribbon board -> viewing hill -> starting arch;
- Story House -> notice board -> village square;
- Shell Cove -> tide pools -> Moonlit Point.

This should be used where random wandering would look aimless.

## 3. Activity loop

The unicorn alternates between movement and a simple location-specific idle/reaction.

Examples:

- walk to flowers, lower head/look, pause, move on;
- approach the fountain, watch it, turn away;
- trot near the race track, pause as if stretching, continue;
- walk to a crystal, listen/watch its glow, move away;
- inspect shells at the beach, then continue along the shore.

These reactions do not need complex animations to work. Facing, short pauses, small existing reaction effects and contextual positioning can sell the behaviour.

## 4. Social pause

Two compatible residents may occasionally occupy nearby authored points and face one another for a short period.

This is ambient presentation only unless a quest specifically uses it.

It should create moments such as:

- two unicorns apparently chatting near the fountain;
- racers standing together near the ribbon board;
- visitors sharing Picnic Hill;
- two beach residents comparing shells.

Do not allow social pauses to form collision walls or block interactive objects.

## 5. Contextual relocation

Some residents should appear in different authored locations depending on progression, selected valley time state, magical weather, active story state or the current scene visit.

This does not require simulating their journey across unloaded maps.

Examples:

- Marigold is sometimes inside the Bakery and sometimes outside arranging something;
- Lumi can appear in Story House or Whispering Woods when relevant;
- Nova can move between Rainbow Run's board, arch and viewing area;
- supporting racers can appear as spectators when not currently racing;
- a friend may visit Moonflower Glade after a relationship milestone;
- Beach residents may move between shoreline and sheltered areas based on the selected atmosphere state.

Relocation must never make required story progress confusing or inaccessible.

---

# Interaction contract

Moving residents must remain as easy to talk to as static residents.

When the player enters interaction range or directly selects/taps a moving unicorn:

1. the resident stops safely;
2. the resident faces the player where practical;
3. normal interaction/talk UI becomes authoritative;
4. dialogue/story behaviour remains unchanged;
5. after interaction ends, the resident waits briefly and then resumes or chooses a sensible next routine point.

A player should never need to chase a quest giver around the map.

For required conversations, introductions and important quest hand-ins, residents may temporarily use an authored anchor state rather than their ambient routine.

---

# Population motion targets

These are presentation targets, not rigid simultaneous-NPC quotas.

## Sunbeam Village

The Village should show the strongest sense of everyday movement.

When enough residents are present, aim for roughly **2-4 independently moving unicorns** across the square/exterior spaces at a time, plus static or temporarily idle residents.

Possible behaviours:

- cross the square;
- visit the fountain;
- approach a shop frontage;
- sit/idle near seating;
- move between the notice board and another landmark;
- enter an authored off-screen/doorway state and later appear elsewhere rather than walking through a wall.

## Moonflower Glade

The Glade remains quieter, but visiting friends/supporting residents should not always materialise at one fixed marker.

Visitors can:

- arrive from the main path;
- wander toward the garden or bridge;
- inspect the display area;
- pause near Moonflower Cottage;
- eventually leave along a valid route.

## Rainbow Meadow and Rainbow Run

Movement should reinforce the energetic social/racing identity.

Examples:

- racers doing gentle warm-up trot routes;
- spectators moving between ribbon board and viewing points;
- picnickers walking between Picnic Hill and pond;
- supporting residents following short butterfly/flower routes.

## Crystal Brook

Movement should be slower and exploratory.

Examples:

- residents strolling along bank-safe paths;
- pausing at singing crystals;
- walking between stepping-stone viewpoints and the grotto approach;
- visitors appearing at different Brook landmarks after progression.

## Whispering Woods

Use fewer moving residents so the Woods remains mysterious rather than crowded.

Examples:

- slow authored patrol between glowing landmarks;
- stopping at mushrooms/fireflies;
- a resident briefly disappearing behind a foreground route and appearing at another valid point;
- contextual relocation rather than constant random motion.

## Starlight Beach

Beach movement should feel loose and playful.

Examples:

- shoreline strolling;
- moving between Shell Cove and tide pools;
- short energetic dashes for racer-type residents;
- stopping to inspect shells or water;
- walking up to Moonlit Point and pausing at the view.

---

# Core-character movement

Autonomous life should not be limited to background residents.

Where it does not compromise story clarity, the established cast can also gain bounded routines:

- **Pip:** curious loops around the Glade and nearby discoveries;
- **Willow:** garden-focused movement between flower patches/nearby points;
- **Marigold:** Bakery interior/exterior and event-preparation movement;
- **Nova:** Rainbow Run board, start area and spectator/warm-up positions;
- **Pebble:** maker/collection locations and occasional region visits;
- **Lumi:** Story House, clue locations and Woods appearances.

Important quest states remain allowed to anchor them temporarily so a child always knows where to find the person she needs.

# Technical design constraints

R6.5-WP2 should implement this as a reusable, data-driven routine layer rather than bespoke update-loop code in every scene.

A resident routine definition should be able to express, where practical:

- allowed scene/location;
- authored safe waypoint IDs or bounded wander zone;
- movement speed/profile;
- pause-duration range;
- route mode: loop, ping-pong, weighted choice or bounded wander;
- contextual idle/reaction at selected points;
- progression/time/weather/story conditions;
- interaction pause/resume behaviour;
- temporary story anchor override;
- fallback point if navigation fails.

The implementation should reuse existing production movement/facing conventions where possible.

Do not create full global NPC simulation. Unloaded scenes do not need residents physically walking in real time. Contextual relocation can be resolved when a scene starts from save/world state and authored conditions.

Ordinary ambient position does not need to be saved every few pixels. Save only state that has actual gameplay meaning.

---

# Navigation and safety rules

Autonomous residents must never make the game harder to control.

Requirements:

- residents stay on authored walkable ground;
- residents do not cross blocked water/scenery simply because the straight-line route is shorter;
- residents do not permanently block doors, gateways, narrow paths, shop interactions or quest objects;
- residents yield or recover if they become stuck;
- a route timeout chooses a safe idle/fallback rather than oscillating forever;
- movement facing remains stable and must not reintroduce the click-navigation direction flicker fixed during R6;
- touch targets remain usable while a resident is moving;
- multiple residents must not create severe collision crowding;
- ambient motion should reduce or simplify where necessary on performance-constrained devices.

If physical NPC collision creates more frustration than value, ambient residents may use non-blocking or softened collision while preserving interaction range and visual layering.

---

# Child-UX rules

Movement should add life without adding cognitive load.

Therefore:

- required NPCs should never become difficult to locate because of autonomous routines;
- quest guidance may point to the resident's current authored area rather than an exact stale coordinate;
- critical dialogue should stop movement immediately;
- residents should not sprint away as the player approaches;
- movement speeds should generally remain slower than or comfortably readable relative to player movement;
- no content should permanently disappear because the player missed a moving resident;
- routines must not depend on the real-world clock.

---

# Acceptance for R6.5-WP2

WP2 is not complete merely because an NPC can technically wander.

Acceptance requires:

- reusable route/wander behaviour driven predominantly by data/configuration;
- interaction reliably pauses/resumes moving residents;
- story/quest anchor override exists;
- at least one resident demonstrates local wander;
- at least one demonstrates a purposeful landmark route;
- at least one demonstrates contextual relocation between sensible positions;
- movement avoids authored blockers/transition areas;
- no repeated facing flicker or stuck oscillation;
- mobile interaction remains easy;
- performance remains acceptable with several residents active;
- automated tests cover route bounds, interaction pause/resume and critical-anchor behaviour where practical.

---

# R6.5 final-gate expectation

By R6.5-WP17, the player should routinely see evidence that other unicorns are living in the valley rather than waiting in place for her.

A healthy final build should include moments such as:

- entering Sunbeam Village and seeing unicorns crossing the square or visiting landmarks;
- arriving at Rainbow Run while racers/spectators are already moving around;
- meeting a familiar supporting resident somewhere different from the previous session;
- seeing a friend arrive at or wander through Moonflower Glade;
- encountering quiet purposeful movement at Crystal Brook or in the Woods;
- watching Beach residents move naturally between its subareas.

The target feeling is simple: **the valley has a life of its own, but it always makes room for the player.**
